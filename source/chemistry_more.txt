2M|The rate of reaction is|the change in concentration per unit time|the total amount of product|the activation energy|the yield|The rate of reaction is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time, measured in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

3W|Why is rate often measured using 1/time for a fixed amount of change?|proportional;rate;inverse|For a fixed amount of reaction (e.g. a set amount of precipitate), rate ∝ 1/time, so 1/t can be used as a measure of the rate.

3M|Which change does NOT increase collision frequency?|adding a catalyst|raising concentration|raising pressure|grinding a solid|A catalyst does not change how often particles collide; it lowers Ea instead, so more of the collisions are successful.
# c-1-6|3.1.6 Chemical equilibria and Kc|Physical Chemistry
1M|Le Chatelier's principle: a system at equilibrium responds to a change by|shifting to oppose the change|reinforcing the change|stopping|speeding up only|Le Chatelier's principle: if a factor affecting an equilibrium changes, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
1M|Raising temperature for an exothermic forward reaction shifts equilibrium|left|right|nowhere|it depends on pressure|Raising the temperature favours the endothermic direction to absorb the heat. If the forward reaction is exothermic, the equilibrium moves left.

2M|Units of Kc for 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ are|mol⁻¹ dm³|mol dm⁻³|no units|mol² dm⁻⁶|Kc = [SO₃]² ÷ ([SO₂]²[O₂]). Units: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = (mol dm⁻³)⁻¹ = mol⁻¹ dm³.
2W|Why does adding more reactant not change Kc?|temperature;only temperature|Kc is only changed by temperature. Adding reactant makes the position shift right until the ratio of concentrations equals Kc again.
2M|For an endothermic forward reaction, increasing temperature makes Kc|increase|decrease|stay the same|become zero|For an endothermic forward reaction, a rise in temperature shifts the equilibrium right, so more products form and Kc increases.
2M|Removing product from an equilibrium mixture|shifts equilibrium right|shifts it left|has no effect|changes Kc|Removing product lowers its concentration. The equilibrium shifts right to oppose the change and replace it.
3M|Increasing pressure on H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI|has no effect on position|shifts right|shifts left|changes Kc|H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI has 2 mol of gas on each side, so changing pressure does not change the position of equilibrium.
3W|Why is a high pressure in industry expensive?|cost;energy;equipment;safety;strong|High pressure needs thick-walled pipes and powerful pumps, which cost a lot to build and run, and uses more energy; there are also safety risks.
3M|In Kc expressions, solids are|left out (concentrations constant)|included|squared|added|The concentration of a pure solid (or liquid) is constant, so it is left out of a heterogeneous Kc expression.
3M|ethanoic acid + ethanol ⇌ ethyl ethanoate + water. Adding water|shifts equilibrium left|shifts it right|has no effect|changes Kc|Water is a product. Adding it shifts the equilibrium left to oppose the change, so less ester is present; Kc is unchanged.
3M|If Kc is very large, the equilibrium mixture contains|mostly products|mostly reactants|equal amounts|no products|A very large Kc means the numerator (products) is much bigger than the denominator, so the position of equilibrium lies far to the right.

# c-1-8|3.1.8 Thermodynamics|Physical Chemistry

# c-1-9|3.1.9 Rate equations|Physical Chemistry

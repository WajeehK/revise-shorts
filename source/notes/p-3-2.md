@intro Forces change motion. This topic covers net force, drag and terminal velocity, moments and equilibrium, and pressure in fluids, including upthrust.
# Net force and weight
- **Newton's second law** (simple form): $F=ma$, where F is the **net** force. 1 N is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻².
- **Weight**, $W=mg$, is the gravitational force on a mass.
- Other forces you need to know: **tension**, **normal contact force**, **upthrust**, **friction** and **drag**.
- A **free-body diagram** shows all the forces acting on one object, drawn as labelled arrows from the object.
!fig p-slope-fbd Free-body diagram for a block on a slope
# Drag and terminal velocity
- Drag increases with speed. It also depends on the cross-sectional area, the shape and the density of the fluid.
- When an object falls, initially only weight acts, so a = g. As its speed increases, drag increases, the net force decreases, and the acceleration decreases.
- When **drag = weight**, the net force is zero and the object falls at **terminal velocity**.
!fig p-terminal Velocity–time graph for a falling object reaching terminal velocity
:::ex Skydiver
Q: A 75 kg skydiver experiences 450 N of drag. Find her acceleration.
1. Net force = mg − D = 75 × 9.81 − 450 = 736 − 450 = 286 N.
2. a = F/m = 286/75 = 3.8 m s⁻².
A: 3.8 m s⁻² downwards. She reaches terminal velocity when drag is 736 N.
:::
# Moments, couples and equilibrium
:::def
**Moment of a force:** force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force (in N m).
**Couple:** a pair of equal, opposite, parallel forces that produces rotation only. Torque of a couple = one force × the perpendicular distance between the forces.
**Principle of moments:** for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point = the sum of the anticlockwise moments about that point.
**Centre of gravity:** the point at which the whole weight of the body may be taken to act.
:::
!fig p-moments Balancing moments on a beam
- For **equilibrium**, the resultant force is zero **and** the resultant moment is zero.
:::ex Seesaw
Q: A 1.0 m uniform beam weighing 20 N is pivoted at its centre. A 30 N load hangs 0.20 m to the left of the pivot. Where must a 15 N weight hang to balance it?
1. The beam's own weight acts at the pivot, so it has no moment.
2. 30 × 0.20 = 15 × d, so d = 0.40 m.
A: 0.40 m to the right of the pivot.
:::
# Density and pressure
:::eq
$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}\qquad p=\frac{F}{A}\qquad p=h\rho g$$
:::
- The pressure in a fluid increases with depth: $\Delta p=h\rho g$.
- **Upthrust** arises because the pressure at the bottom of an object is greater than the pressure at the top.
- **Archimedes' principle:** upthrust = the weight of fluid displaced.
:::ex Upthrust
Q: A 0.0020 m³ block is fully submerged in water (ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³). Find the upthrust.
1. Mass of water displaced = 1000 × 0.0020 = 2.0 kg.
2. Upthrust = 2.0 × 9.81 = 19.6 N.
A: About 20 N.
:::
:::warn
- Measuring the distance for a moment along the beam when the force is at an angle. The distance must be **perpendicular** to the line of action.
- Forgetting the weight of the beam itself when it is not pivoted at its centre.
- Saying that drag decreases as speed increases.
:::
:::tip
- Take moments about the point where an unknown force acts. That force then has no moment and drops out of the equation.
:::
:::sum
- F = ma for the net force; W = mg.
- Terminal velocity is reached when drag = weight.
- Moment = F × perpendicular distance. In equilibrium, the net force and net moment are both zero.
- p = F/A, p = hρg, and upthrust = weight of fluid displaced.
:::

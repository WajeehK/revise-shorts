@intro Astrophysics uses the light from stars to work out what they are made of, how hot and large they are, and how far away they are. Cosmology uses the same evidence to tell the story of the whole Universe, from the Big Bang onwards.
# Objects in the Universe
- Planets, planetary satellites (moons), comets, solar systems, galaxies and the Universe.
# The life of a star
- **Formation:** gravity pulls a nebula of gas and dust together. The gravitational potential energy becomes thermal energy, forming a protostar. When the core is hot enough, **hydrogen fusion** starts.
- **Main sequence:** the outward radiation and gas pressure balances the inward pull of gravity.
- A **low-mass star** (about 0.5–10 solar masses) becomes a **red giant**, then sheds its outer layers as a planetary nebula, leaving a **white dwarf**. Electron degeneracy pressure supports a white dwarf, provided its mass is below the **Chandrasekhar limit** of 1.44 solar masses.
- A **massive star** becomes a **red supergiant**, then a **supernova**. It leaves a **neutron star** or, above about 3 solar masses, a **black hole**.
- The **Hertzsprung–Russell diagram** plots luminosity against surface temperature, with temperature **increasing to the left**. It shows the main sequence, red giants and supergiants (top right) and white dwarfs (bottom left).
# Energy levels and spectra
!fig p-energy-levels Electron energy levels: a transition emits a photon with hf = ΔE
- Electrons in atoms have **discrete energy levels**. Energies are negative, and 0 is the energy of a free electron.
- When an electron moves down a level, it emits a photon with $hf=\Delta E$.
- Hot dense sources give **continuous spectra**. Hot low-density gases give **emission line spectra**. Cool gas in front of a continuous source gives **absorption line spectra**, which have dark lines.
- A star's absorption lines identify the elements in its atmosphere. A **diffraction grating**, $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$, measures the wavelengths of the lines.
:::ex Photon from an energy-level transition
Q: An electron moves from the −1.51 eV level to the −3.40 eV level. Find the wavelength of the emitted photon.
1. ΔE = 1.89 eV = 1.89 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
2. λ = hc/ΔE = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹.
A: λ = 6.58 × 10⁻⁷ m (red light, the H-alpha line).
:::
# Luminosity and temperature
:::eq
$$\lambda_\text{max}T=\text{constant}=2.9\times10^{-3}\text{ m K}\ \ (\text{Wien})\qquad L=4\pi r^2\sigma T^4\ \ (\text{Stefan})$$
:::
- **Luminosity** is the total power output of a star. σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴.
- Hotter stars peak at shorter wavelengths, so they appear bluer.
:::ex Radius of a star
Q: A star has λ_max = 290 nm and L = 4.0 × 10²⁸ W. Find its surface temperature and radius.
1. T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 2.9 × 10⁻⁷ = 1.0 × 10⁴ K.
2. r = √(L / 4πσT⁴) = √(4.0 × 10²⁸ / (4π × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 10¹⁶)).
A: T = 10 000 K and r = 2.4 × 10⁹ m
:::
# Distances and cosmology
- **Units:** the astronomical unit (1 AU = 1.50 × 10¹¹ m), the light-year (9.46 × 10¹⁵ m) and the parsec (3.1 × 10¹⁶ m).
- **Stellar parallax:** d (in pc) = 1/p (in arcseconds).
- **Doppler effect:** $\Delta\lambda/\lambda\approx\Delta f/f\approx v/c$. Light from receding galaxies is **redshifted**.
- **Hubble's law:** $v=H_0d$. The Universe is expanding, and its age is approximately **1/H₀**.
- **The cosmological principle:** on a large scale, the Universe is **homogeneous and isotropic**, and the laws of physics are the same everywhere.
- **Evidence for the Big Bang:** the redshift of galaxies (Hubble's law), and the **cosmic microwave background radiation**, which is radiation from the early Universe stretched to microwave wavelengths, corresponding to about 2.7 K.
- **Evolution of the Universe:** quarks formed, then protons and neutrons, then nuclei, then atoms, then stars and galaxies.
- **Dark matter** is inferred from galaxy rotation curves. **Dark energy** is inferred from the accelerating expansion.
:::ex Age of the Universe
Q: H₀ = 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. Estimate the age of the Universe.
1. Convert to SI units: H₀ = 70 × 10³ / 3.1 × 10²² = 2.26 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹.
2. Age ≈ 1/H₀ = 4.4 × 10¹⁷ s.
A: About 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years (14 billion years).
:::

- Star life cycles depend on mass, ending in white dwarfs, neutron stars or black holes.
- Line spectra: hf = ΔE; the elements are identified by their absorption lines.
- Wien: λ_max T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ m K. Stefan: L = 4πr²σT⁴.
- Distances use parallax and the parsec. Hubble's law v = H₀d gives an age ≈ 1/H₀. The CMB supports the Big Bang.

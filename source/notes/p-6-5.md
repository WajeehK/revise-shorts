@spec OCR H556 6.5.1 Using X-rays; 6.5.2 Diagnostic methods in medicine; 6.5.3 Using ultrasound
@intro Medical imaging uses physics to look inside the body without surgery. X-rays, CAT scans, radioactive tracers, PET and ultrasound each depend on a different interaction between radiation and matter.
# X-rays
- **Production:** electrons are accelerated through a large p.d. (tens of kV) and hit a **tungsten** target. Their deceleration produces X-ray photons, with a maximum energy equal to eV. Only about 1% of the energy becomes X-rays; the rest becomes heat, so the anode is rotated and cooled.
:::eq
$$I=I_0e^{-\mu x}$$
:::
- μ is the **attenuation (absorption) coefficient**. Bone has a much higher μ than soft tissue, which gives contrast on the image.
| Attenuation mechanism | Photon energy | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| simple scatter | 1–20 keV | the photon bounces off an electron with no energy change |
| photoelectric effect | below about 100 keV | the photon is absorbed and an electron is ejected |
| Compton scattering | 0.5–5 MeV | the photon loses part of its energy to an electron and is scattered |
| pair production | above 1.02 MeV | the photon becomes an electron–positron pair |
- **Contrast media**, such as barium sulfate or iodine, have a high atomic number and absorb X-rays strongly. They outline soft tissues such as the gut and blood vessels.
- **CAT scans:** an X-ray tube rotates around the patient, and many detectors record slices. A computer builds a 3D image. CAT scans give better detail than an X-ray image, but a higher dose.
:::ex X-ray attenuation
Q: For a beam of X-rays in soft tissue, μ = 0.35 cm⁻¹. What fraction of the intensity is transmitted through 4.0 cm?
1. I/I₀ = e^(−0.35 × 4.0) = e^(−1.4).
A: 0.25, so 25% is transmitted.
:::
# Diagnostic methods using radioactive tracers
- **Medical tracers** are radioactive substances that are injected or swallowed, and that collect in target organs. **Technetium-99m** emits only γ rays, has a 6-hour half-life, and is easily attached to other molecules. **Fluorine-18** (in fluorodeoxyglucose) emits positrons and is used for PET.
- **Gamma camera:** a **collimator** (lead tubes) lets through only the γ rays travelling parallel to the tubes. A **scintillator** (NaI crystal) converts each γ photon into light. **Photomultiplier tubes** turn the light into electrical pulses, and a computer forms the image.
- **PET scans:** an emitted positron annihilates with an electron, producing **two 511 keV γ photons travelling in opposite directions**. Detectors around the patient time the arrivals of each pair to locate the source. PET shows **function** (for example, metabolism), not just structure.
:::ex Annihilation energy
Q: Show that each photon from positron–electron annihilation has an energy of about 0.51 MeV.
1. Mass of each particle = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, so the total energy is 2mc² = 2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × (3.0 × 10⁸)² = 1.64 × 10⁻¹³ J.
2. This is shared between two photons: 8.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ J each, which is 0.51 MeV.
A: About 0.51 MeV per photon.
:::
# Ultrasound
- Ultrasound has frequencies above 20 kHz; medical imaging uses 1–15 MHz. It is produced and detected using the **piezoelectric effect**: a p.d. across a crystal makes it change shape, and the reverse also happens.
:::eq
$$Z=\rho c\qquad\frac{I_r}{I_0}=\frac{(Z_2-Z_1)^2}{(Z_2+Z_1)^2}$$
:::
- **Acoustic impedance**, Z, is density × speed of sound. A large difference in Z gives strong reflection. Between air and skin almost all the ultrasound is reflected, so **coupling gel** is used, because its Z is close to that of skin.
- An **A-scan** measures the depth of a boundary from the echo time. A **B-scan** sweeps the probe to build a 2D image.
- **Doppler imaging** measures the speed of blood flow from the frequency shift: Δf = 2fv cos θ / c.
:::ex Reflection at a boundary
Q: Find the fraction of ultrasound intensity reflected at a boundary between muscle (Z = 1.70 × 10⁶) and bone (Z = 7.80 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹).
1. I_r/I₀ = (7.80 − 1.70)² / (7.80 + 1.70)² = 37.2 / 90.3.
A: 0.41, so about 41% is reflected.
:::
- **The X-ray tube.** Its basic structure and components: a heater (cathode) emits electrons by thermionic emission; a high voltage supply accelerates them to the anode; they hit a target metal (tungsten), producing X-rays and a lot of heat.
- **CAT scanning.** Computerised axial tomography uses a rotating X-ray tube producing a thin fan-shaped X-ray beam, a ring of detectors, and software that builds a 3D image for display from many slices.
- **Ultrasound** is a longitudinal wave with a frequency greater than 20 kHz (above human hearing).
:::warn
- Saying that X-ray contrast media are "radioactive". They simply absorb X-rays strongly.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 in the Doppler equation and the echo-time calculation (the pulse travels there and back).
- Confusing PET (positrons and two photons) with the gamma camera (single γ photons).
:::
:::tip
- Compare the methods by what they show (structure or function), the dose (ultrasound is non-ionising), the cost and the resolution.
:::
:::sum
- X-rays: I = I₀e^(−μx); simple scatter, the photoelectric effect, Compton scattering and pair production; contrast media; CAT scans.
- Tracers: Tc-99m with a gamma camera; F-18 with PET (two 511 keV photons).
- Ultrasound: piezoelectric transducers; Z = ρc; gel for matching impedance; A- and B-scans; Doppler.
:::

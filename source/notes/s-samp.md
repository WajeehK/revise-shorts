@spec Edexcel 9MA0 Statistics topic 1: 1.1 populations and samples, sampling techniques, critiquing sampling
@intro Statistics starts with collecting data. This topic covers the difference between a population and a sample, the main sampling methods with their strengths and weaknesses, and how to critique the way a sample was taken.
# Populations and samples
:::def
**Population:** the whole set of items that are of interest.
**Census:** a measurement or observation of every member of the population.
**Sample:** a selection of observations taken from a subset of the population, used to find out about the population.
**Sampling unit:** an individual member of the population that can be sampled.
**Sampling frame:** a list of the sampling units, used to select the sample.
:::
| Census | Sample |
|---|---|
| fully accurate (for that population) | cheaper and quicker |
| time-consuming and expensive | less data to process |
| impossible if testing destroys the item | may not represent the population; natural variation between samples |
- A larger sample generally gives more reliable estimates, but it costs more. Different samples from the same population can give different results; this is **natural variation**.
# Random sampling methods
:::def
**Simple random sampling:** every sample of size $n$ has an equal chance of being chosen. Number the sampling frame, then use random numbers.
**Systematic sampling:** choose every $k$th item from an ordered list, starting from a random point between 1 and $k$, where $k=\frac{N}{n}$.
**Stratified sampling:** split the population into groups (strata) and take a simple random sample from each group, in proportion to the group's size.
:::
:::ex Stratified sample sizes
Q: A school has 400 students in Year 12 and 350 in Year 13. A stratified sample of 30 is needed. How many should come from each year?
1. Total = 750. Year 12: $\frac{400}{750}\times30=16$.
2. Year 13: $\frac{350}{750}\times30=14$.
A: 16 from Year 12 and 14 from Year 13.
:::
# Non-random sampling methods
:::def
**Quota sampling:** an interviewer selects people to fill quotas that reflect the population (for example, so many men aged 20–30). No sampling frame is needed.
**Opportunity (convenience) sampling:** the sample is taken from people who are available at the time and fit the criteria.
:::
| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| simple random | free of bias; easy for small populations | needs a sampling frame; awkward for large populations |
| systematic | simple and quick; suits large populations | needs a frame; bias if the list has a pattern |
| stratified | reflects the population structure; gives estimates for each stratum | needs the strata to be clearly defined; can still be costly |
| quota | quick, cheap, no frame needed; reflects groups | non-random, so possible interviewer bias; non-response is not recorded |
| opportunity | very easy and cheap | unlikely to be representative; highly dependent on the person sampling |
:::ex Critiquing a sample
Q: A researcher surveys the first 50 shoppers leaving a supermarket at 10 am on a Tuesday to find out about the shopping habits of the town. Identify the method and two problems.
1. This is opportunity sampling.
2. Shoppers at 10 am on a weekday are mostly people who don't work in the daytime (for example, retired people), so the sample is unlikely to represent the whole town.
3. Only people at one supermarket are surveyed, and that excludes people who shop elsewhere or online.
A: An opportunity sample, which is biased by its time and location. Improve it by sampling at different times and places, or by using stratified sampling.
:::
# The large data set
- Edexcel's **large data set** gives weather data from several UK and overseas stations for May–October of 1987 and 2015. Check with your teacher which version your exam uses. Questions expect you to know its variables, such as daily mean temperature, rainfall, sunshine, wind speed and direction, humidity and cloud cover, and its quirks: "tr" means a trace of rain (less than 0.05 mm), and "n/a" means data is not available.
- You may be asked to choose a sampling method for the large data set, or to clean the data, for example by removing n/a values.
:::warn
- Calling systematic sampling "random" without mentioning the random **start**.
- Rounding stratified sample sizes so that they no longer add up to the total.
- Saying that a bigger sample "removes bias". It reduces variability, but a biased method stays biased.
:::
:::tip
- When critiquing, always link your comment to the **context** of the question (who is excluded, and why that matters).
:::
:::sum
- A census covers the whole population; a sample is a subset.
- Random methods: simple random, systematic, stratified. Non-random methods: quota, opportunity.
- Know at least one advantage and one disadvantage of each method, and critique samples in context.
:::

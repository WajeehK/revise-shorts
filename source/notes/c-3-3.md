# Halogenoalkanes
- C–X is polar (C is δ+). Reactivity depends on **bond enthalpy**: C–I is the weakest, so it is hydrolysed fastest.
# Nucleophilic substitution
- OH⁻ (warm NaOH(aq), reflux) gives an alcohol. CN⁻ (KCN in ethanol/water) gives a nitrile, adding one carbon. NH₃ (excess, in ethanol, under pressure) gives a primary amine.
- Mechanism: the nucleophile's lone pair attacks the δ+ C, and the C–X bond breaks heterolytically, releasing X⁻.

# Elimination
- KOH in **ethanol**, hot: OH⁻ acts as a **base**, removes H⁺ from the adjacent carbon, forms C=C, and X⁻ leaves.
- Tertiary halogenoalkanes favour elimination; primary ones favour substitution.
# Rate experiment
- Water + AgNO₃ in ethanol at the same temperature: time how long the precipitate takes to form (I fastest, then Br, then Cl).
# Exam tips
> In mechanisms, draw the lone pair on the nucleophile and the δ+/δ− dipole on C–X. Arrows start **at** the lone pair or bond.

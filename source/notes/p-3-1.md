@intro Kinematics describes how things move: displacement, velocity and acceleration. It does not ask why they move. This topic covers motion graphs, the suvat equations, experiments to find g, stopping distances and projectiles.
# Key quantities
:::def
**Displacement (s):** distance moved in a given direction (a vector, in m).
**Velocity (v):** rate of change of displacement, $v=\Delta s/\Delta t$ (a vector, in m s⁻¹).
**Speed:** rate of change of distance (a scalar). **Instantaneous speed** is the speed at a particular instant; **average speed** = total distance ÷ total time.
**Acceleration (a):** rate of change of velocity, $a=\Delta v/\Delta t$ (in m s⁻²).
:::
# Motion graphs
!fig p-vt-graph Velocity–time graph: the gradient is acceleration and the area is displacement
| Graph | Gradient | Area under the graph |
|---|---|---|
| displacement–time | velocity | — |
| velocity–time | acceleration | displacement |
| acceleration–time | — | change in velocity |
- For a curved displacement–time graph, draw a **tangent** to find the instantaneous velocity.
# The suvat equations (constant acceleration only)
:::eq
$$v=u+at$$
$$s=\tfrac12(u+v)t$$
$$s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$$
$$v^2=u^2+2as$$
:::
:::ex Braking car
Q: A car travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ brakes uniformly to rest in 50 m. Find the deceleration and the braking time.
1. v² = u² + 2as gives 0 = 625 + 2a(50), so a = −6.25 m s⁻².
2. v = u + at gives 0 = 25 − 6.25t, so t = 4.0 s.
A: The deceleration is 6.25 m s⁻² and the time is 4.0 s.
:::
# Free fall and determining g
- In free fall near the Earth's surface with no air resistance, the acceleration is **g = 9.81 m s⁻²**, the same for all masses.
:::prac PAG 1: determining g
- Drop a ball through a measured height h, using **light gates** or an electromagnet with a timer, or record it with a video camera and frame-by-frame analysis.
- Since $h=\tfrac12gt^2$, plot h against t²: the gradient is g/2.
- Alternatively, use two light gates and $v^2=u^2+2gh$.
- Reduce uncertainty by using larger heights, repeating each drop, and using a dense, small ball to reduce drag.
:::
# Stopping distances
- **Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance.**
- **Thinking distance** = speed × reaction time. It is increased by tiredness, alcohol, drugs and distractions.
- **Braking distance** is proportional to v², from v² = 2as. It is increased by wet or icy roads, worn tyres or brakes, and greater mass.
:::ex Stopping distance
Q: A driver with a reaction time of 0.70 s is travelling at 20 m s⁻¹. The car decelerates at 5.0 m s⁻². Find the stopping distance.
1. Thinking distance = 20 × 0.70 = 14 m.
2. Braking distance = v²/2a = 400/10 = 40 m.
A: Stopping distance = 54 m.
:::
# Projectile motion
!fig p-projectile The horizontal and vertical components of a projectile are independent
- The horizontal and vertical motions are **independent**. Horizontally there is no force (ignoring air resistance), so the velocity is **constant**. Vertically, the acceleration is **g downwards**.
- Resolve the launch velocity: $u_x=u\cos\theta$ and $u_y=u\sin\theta$. The time of flight comes from the vertical motion.
:::ex Horizontal launch
Q: A ball rolls off a 1.25 m high table at 3.0 m s⁻¹. How far from the table does it land?
1. Vertically: 1.25 = ½ × 9.81 × t², so t = 0.505 s.
2. Horizontally: x = 3.0 × 0.505 = 1.5 m.
A: About 1.5 m from the table.
:::
- The equations of motion apply to constant acceleration in a straight line, including bodies falling in a uniform gravitational field without air resistance (a = g).
:::prac Investigating motion and collisions
Techniques and procedures used to investigate the motion and collisions of objects include gliders on an air track, trolleys, ticker timers, light gates, data-loggers and video techniques. A light gate measures the time a card of known length takes to pass, giving a speed; two light gates or a double-interrupt card give an acceleration.
:::
:::warn
- Using the suvat equations when the acceleration is not constant.
- Giving vertical acceleration the same sign as upward velocity. Choose a positive direction and stick to it.
- Thinking a projectile's horizontal speed decreases (when air resistance is ignored).
:::
:::tip
- List s, u, v, a and t, mark the unknown, and choose the equation that leaves out the quantity you don't need.
:::
:::sum
- On a velocity–time graph, the gradient is acceleration and the area is displacement.
- The suvat equations apply only to constant acceleration; g = 9.81 m s⁻².
- Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance; braking distance ∝ v².
- In projectiles, horizontal velocity is constant and vertical acceleration is g.
:::

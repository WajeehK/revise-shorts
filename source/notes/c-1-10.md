# Kp
- Partial pressure $p_A=x_AP$, where the mole fraction is $x_A=\frac{n_A}{n_{total}}$.
For $a\text{A}(g)\rightleftharpoons b\text{B}(g)$:
$$K_p=\frac{(p_B)^b}{(p_A)^a}$$
- Only gases appear in $K_p$ (in heterogeneous equilibria, leave out the solids).
- Only temperature changes $K_p$. Changing the pressure moves the position, but $K_p$ stays the same.
# Worked example
N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ at 100 kPa, with mole fractions 0.6 and 0.4: $p_{NO_2}=40$ and $p_{N_2O_4}=60$, so $K_p=\frac{40^2}{60}=26.7$ kPa.
# Worked example 2 (from moles)
2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ at a total pressure of 200 kPa. At equilibrium there are 0.20 mol SO₂, 0.10 mol O₂ and 1.70 mol SO₃ (2.00 mol in total).
- Mole fractions are 0.10, 0.05 and 0.85, so the partial pressures are 20, 10 and 170 kPa.
$$K_p=\frac{170^2}{20^2\times10}=7.2\text{ kPa}^{-1}$$
# Effect of conditions
- If the forward reaction is exothermic, raising $T$ makes $K_p$ smaller, so the yield falls.
- Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change the partial pressures, so the position does not move.
# Exam tips
> Check that the mole fractions sum to 1 and give the units of $K_p$ (kPa raised to some power, or none).

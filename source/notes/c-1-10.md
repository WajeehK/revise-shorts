@intro For reactions between gases it is natural to use pressures instead of concentrations. This topic covers mole fractions, partial pressures, writing Kp expressions, calculating Kp and its units, and how conditions affect Kp.
# Mole fraction and partial pressure
:::def
**Mole fraction, x:** the number of moles of a gas divided by the total number of moles of gas.
**Partial pressure, p:** the pressure a gas would exert if it alone occupied the whole volume. It equals mole fraction × total pressure.
:::
:::eq
$$x_A=\frac{n_A}{n_{total}}\qquad p_A=x_A\times P_{total}\qquad \sum p=P_{total}$$
:::
- The mole fractions of all the gases add up to 1.
# The equilibrium constant Kp
For $a\text{A(g)}+b\text{B(g)}\rightleftharpoons c\text{C(g)}+d\text{D(g)}$:
$$K_p=\frac{(p_C)^c(p_D)^d}{(p_A)^a(p_B)^b}$$
- Only **gases** are included. Kp is for **homogeneous** gas equilibria. Use round brackets or $p$, not square brackets.
- Units: substitute kPa (or Pa or atm) for each term and cancel.
:::ex Calculating Kp
Q: For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), 1.00 mol of N₂O₄ is heated in a sealed container until 0.40 mol has dissociated. The total pressure is 150 kPa. Calculate Kp.
1. Equilibrium moles: N₂O₄ is $1.00-0.40=0.60$; NO₂ is $2\times0.40=0.80$. The total is 1.40.
2. Mole fractions: N₂O₄ $=0.60/1.40=0.429$; NO₂ $=0.80/1.40=0.571$.
3. Partial pressures: N₂O₄ $=64.3$ kPa; NO₂ $=85.7$ kPa.
4. $K_p=\frac{85.7^2}{64.3}=114$.
A: Kp ≈ 114 kPa
:::
:::ex Using Kp to find a partial pressure
Q: For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), Kp = 50 at a certain temperature. At equilibrium p(H₂) = p(I₂) = 20 kPa. Find p(HI).
1. $50=\frac{p_{HI}^2}{20\times20}$, so $p_{HI}^2=20000$.
2. $p_{HI}=\sqrt{20000}$.
A: p(HI) ≈ 141 kPa (Kp has no units here)
:::
# Homogeneous systems and links to Kc
- A **homogeneous** equilibrium has all species in the same phase (here, all gases). Kp is used for these systems at A-level.
- Kp and Kc describe the same equilibrium. Kp uses partial pressures because, for gases, pressure is easy to measure and is proportional to concentration at a fixed temperature ($p=\frac{n}{V}RT$).
- The more product there is at equilibrium, the larger Kp. A value of Kp much greater than 1 means the equilibrium lies well to the right.
:::ex Partial pressures from a percentage
Q: The Haber process gives an equilibrium mixture of 15% NH₃, 21% N₂ and 64% H₂ by moles at a total pressure of 20 000 kPa. Write the expression for Kp and calculate it.
1. $K_p=\frac{p_{NH_3}^2}{p_{N_2}\,p_{H_2}^3}$
2. Partial pressures: NH₃ $=0.15\times20000=3000$ kPa; N₂ $=4200$ kPa; H₂ $=12800$ kPa.
3. $K_p=\frac{3000^2}{4200\times12800^3}=\frac{9.0\times10^6}{8.81\times10^{15}}=1.0\times10^{-9}$.
4. Units: $\frac{\text{kPa}^2}{\text{kPa}^4}=\text{kPa}^{-2}$.
A: Kp ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ kPa⁻²
:::
# Effect of changing conditions
- **Temperature is the only factor that changes Kp.** If the forward reaction is exothermic, increasing the temperature decreases Kp, and the equilibrium shifts left to restore the ratio. If it is endothermic, Kp increases.
- **Changing the total pressure** changes the partial pressures, but Kp stays the same. The equilibrium position shifts (Le Chatelier) until the expression equals Kp again.
- Example: for N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ (endothermic forward), heating increases Kp, so the mixture becomes browner because there is more NO₂. Increasing the pressure moves the equilibrium to the left (fewer gas moles), but Kp is unchanged.
- A **catalyst** has no effect on Kp or the equilibrium position; equilibrium is only reached faster.
:::warn
- Including solids or liquids in Kp.
- Forgetting that the dissociation of one mole of N₂O₄ makes **two** moles of NO₂ when you calculate the equilibrium moles.
- Using the initial total moles instead of the equilibrium total to find mole fractions.
:::
:::tip
- Set up a table with rows for initial moles, change, equilibrium moles, mole fraction and partial pressure. It keeps the method marks safe.
- Check that the partial pressures add up to the total pressure.
:::
:::sum
- Mole fraction = n/n(total). Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure.
- Kp uses partial pressures of gases only. Find the units by cancelling.
- Only temperature changes Kp. Pressure and catalysts do not.
:::

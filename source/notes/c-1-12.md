# Definitions
- Brønsted–Lowry acid: proton donor. Base: proton acceptor.
$$\text{pH}=-\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]$$
$$K_w=[\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-]=1.0\times10^{-14}\ (298\text{ K})$$
- Pure water is always neutral: [H⁺] = [OH⁻], even when the pH is not 7 (e.g. at higher $T$).
# Weak acids
$$K_a=\frac{[\text{H}^+][\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$$
$$[\text{H}^+]\approx\sqrt{K_ac}$$
$$pK_a=-\log K_a$$
- At half-neutralisation, pH = p$K_a$.
# Buffers
- A buffer resists pH change when small amounts of acid or base are added. Acidic buffer: a weak acid plus its salt (e.g. CH₃COOH / CH₃COONa).
$$[\text{H}^+]=K_a\frac{[\text{HA}]}{[\text{A}^-]}$$
- Blood is buffered by H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻.
# Titration curves
- Indicators: methyl orange (pH 3–4.5) for strong acid–weak base; phenolphthalein (pH 8–10) for weak acid–strong base. There is no sharp end-point for weak–weak titrations.

# Worked example
0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid with $K_a=1.74\times10^{-5}$: $[\text{H}^+]=1.32\times10^{-3}$, so pH = 2.88.
# Exam tips
> For a strong base, find [OH⁻] first, then $[\text{H}^+]=\frac{K_w}{[\text{OH}^-]}$. For a diprotic strong acid, $[\text{H}^+]=2c$.

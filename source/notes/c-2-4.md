@intro Across Period 3 the elements change from metals to non-metals, and their oxides change from basic to acidic. This topic covers the reactions of the elements with water and oxygen, and the structure, bonding and acid–base behaviour of the oxides.
# Reactions of sodium and magnesium with water
- **Sodium** reacts vigorously with cold water. It melts into a ball, fizzes and moves about: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂. The solution formed has pH 13–14.
- **Magnesium** reacts very slowly with cold water, forming Mg(OH)₂, which is only slightly soluble (pH about 9–10). It burns in steam: Mg + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂.
# Reactions with oxygen
| Element | Observation | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Na | yellow flame, white solid | 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O |
| Mg | brilliant white flame, white solid | 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO |
| Al | white flame, white solid | 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃ |
| Si | burns when heated strongly | Si + O₂ → SiO₂ |
| P | white flame, white fumes | P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀ |
| S | blue flame, choking gas | S + O₂ → SO₂ |
- In each oxide the element shows its **highest oxidation state** (equal to its group number), except S, which forms SO₂ (+4). SO₃ (+6) needs a catalyst.
# Structure and melting points of the oxides
| Oxide | Structure and bonding | Melting point |
|---|---|---|
| Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃ | giant ionic lattice | high; MgO is highest because the Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions carry high charges and are small |
| SiO₂ | macromolecular (giant covalent) | very high |
| P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃ | simple molecular | low; only van der Waals forces and dipole–dipole forces between molecules are overcome |
- Al₂O₃ has a lower melting point than MgO even though Al³⁺ has a higher charge. The very small, highly charged Al³⁺ ion polarises the oxide ion, giving the bonds some **covalent character**.
# Oxides with water
| Oxide | With water | pH |
|---|---|---|
| Na₂O | Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH | 13–14 |
| MgO | MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ (slightly soluble) | about 9–10 |
| Al₂O₃, SiO₂ | insoluble | 7 |
| P₄O₁₀ | P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄ | 0–1 |
| SO₂ | SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ | 2–3 |
| SO₃ | SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ | 0–1 |
- **Ionic** oxides contain O²⁻ ions, which react with water to form OH⁻, so they are **basic**. **Covalent** oxides form acids, so they are **acidic**.
# Acid–base character
- **Basic oxides** (Na₂O, MgO) react with acids: MgO + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O.
- **Amphoteric** Al₂O₃ reacts with both acids and bases:
$$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3+6\text{HCl}\rightarrow2\text{AlCl}_3+3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
$$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3+2\text{NaOH}+3\text{H}_2\text{O}\rightarrow2\text{NaAl(OH)}_4$$
- **Acidic oxides** (SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃) react with bases. For example, SiO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + H₂O (with hot, concentrated NaOH); P₄O₁₀ + 12NaOH → 4Na₃PO₄ + 6H₂O; SO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + H₂O.
:::ex Explaining the pH of an oxide solution
Q: Explain why sodium oxide forms a strongly alkaline solution but phosphorus(V) oxide forms a strongly acidic one.
1. Na₂O is ionic. Its O²⁻ ions react with water: O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻.
2. P₄O₁₀ is simple molecular and covalent. It reacts with water to form phosphoric(V) acid, which releases H⁺ ions.
A: Ionic oxides give OH⁻ ions (pH 14); covalent non-metal oxides give acids (pH about 0).
:::
:::ex Writing a neutralisation equation
Q: Write an equation for the reaction of sulfur trioxide with sodium hydroxide.
1. SO₃ is an acidic oxide. With water it gives H₂SO₄, which is neutralised by 2NaOH.
A: SO₃ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + H₂O
:::
:::warn
- Saying that SiO₂ is acidic "because it dissolves in water to form an acid". It is insoluble; it is acidic because it reacts with bases.
- Writing P₂O₅ instead of the molecular formula P₄O₁₀.
- Forgetting that Al₂O₃ is amphoteric. You need an equation with an acid and an equation with a base.
:::
:::tip
- Link the structure to the property: ionic oxides are basic and have high melting points; covalent oxides are acidic. Mention the oxide ion reacting with water.
:::
:::sum
- Na reacts vigorously and Mg slowly with water. All the elements burn in O₂.
- Oxides: Na₂O, MgO and Al₂O₃ are ionic; SiO₂ is giant covalent; P₄O₁₀, SO₂ and SO₃ are simple molecular.
- Character: basic (Na, Mg), amphoteric (Al), acidic (Si, P, S). The pH of oxide solutions falls from 14 to about 0 across the period.
:::

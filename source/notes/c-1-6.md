@intro Many reactions are reversible and reach a dynamic equilibrium. This topic covers Le Chatelier's principle, industrial compromise conditions, and the equilibrium constant Kc, including writing expressions, calculating values and finding units.
# Dynamic equilibrium
:::def
**Dynamic equilibrium:** in a closed system, the forward and reverse reactions happen at the **same rate**, so the concentrations of reactants and products stay **constant**.
:::
- Both reactions are still going on. The concentrations are constant, but they are not usually equal.
- Equilibrium can only be reached in a **closed system**, where nothing can escape.
!fig c-equilibrium Concentrations levelling off as equilibrium is reached
# Le Chatelier's principle
:::def
**Le Chatelier's principle:** if a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose (minimise) that change.
:::
| Change | Effect on position of equilibrium |
|---|---|
| increase the concentration of a reactant | shifts right, using up the added reactant |
| increase the pressure (gases) | shifts to the side with **fewer gas moles** |
| increase the temperature | shifts in the **endothermic** direction |
| add a catalyst | **no change**; equilibrium is reached faster |
- If both sides have the same number of gas moles, pressure has no effect on the position.
:::ex Predicting a shift
Q: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict the effect on the yield of NH₃ of (a) increasing the pressure and (b) increasing the temperature.
1. (a) The left side has 4 gas moles and the right side has 2. Higher pressure shifts equilibrium to the side with fewer moles, so it shifts right.
2. (b) The forward reaction is exothermic, so the reverse reaction is endothermic. Higher temperature shifts equilibrium in the endothermic direction, so it shifts left.
A: (a) The yield increases. (b) The yield decreases.
:::
# Industrial compromise
- **Ethanol by hydration of ethene:** C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g), ΔH = −46 kJ mol⁻¹. Typical conditions are 300 °C, 6–7 MPa and a phosphoric acid catalyst. A lower temperature would give a better yield but the rate would be too slow. A higher pressure would give a better yield but costs more and needs stronger equipment. Unreacted ethene is recycled.
- **Methanol:** CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g), ΔH is negative. The same kind of compromise applies.
- The **compromise** balances a good yield, a fast rate, cost and safety.
# The equilibrium constant Kc
For $a\text{A}+b\text{B}\rightleftharpoons c\text{C}+d\text{D}$ (all in the same phase):
$$K_c=\frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}$$
- Square brackets mean the equilibrium concentration in mol dm⁻³.
- **Units.** Substitute mol dm⁻³ for each term and cancel. Kc can have no units.
- **Only temperature changes Kc.** If the forward reaction is exothermic, raising the temperature decreases Kc. Changing a concentration or the pressure moves the position of equilibrium, but Kc stays the same. A catalyst does not affect Kc.
:::ex Calculating Kc from equilibrium moles
Q: 1.00 mol of ethanoic acid and 1.00 mol of ethanol reach equilibrium in 0.500 dm³. At equilibrium 0.667 mol of ethyl ethanoate has formed. Calculate Kc for CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
1. Equilibrium moles: ester 0.667 and water 0.667. The acid and the alcohol each have $1.00-0.667=0.333$.
2. Concentrations: divide by 0.500. The volumes cancel here because there are equal numbers of moles on each side.
3. $K_c=\frac{0.667\times0.667}{0.333\times0.333}=4.01$.
A: Kc ≈ 4.0 (no units)
:::
:::ex Finding units
Q: Write the expression for Kc for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) and find its units.
1. $K_c=\frac{[\text{SO}_3]^2}{[\text{SO}_2]^2[\text{O}_2]}$
2. Units: $\frac{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^2}{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^3}=(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^{-1}$.
A: mol⁻¹ dm³
:::
:::warn
- Saying that a catalyst increases the yield. It only speeds up how quickly equilibrium is reached.
- Using initial moles instead of **equilibrium** moles in Kc. Set up a table with rows for initial, change and equilibrium.
- Saying Kc changes when the pressure or a concentration changes. Only temperature changes Kc.
:::
:::tip
- For a Le Chatelier answer, state the change, the direction of the shift, and the reason (for example "to the side with fewer gas moles, to reduce the pressure").
- Answers about industrial conditions should mention yield, rate, cost of energy or equipment, and safety.
:::
:::sum
- Dynamic equilibrium: equal rates and constant concentrations in a closed system.
- Le Chatelier: the system opposes the change. Pressure favours fewer gas moles; temperature favours the endothermic direction; catalysts have no effect on the position.
- Kc = products over reactants, each raised to its coefficient. Work out the units by cancelling.
- Only temperature changes the value of Kc.
:::

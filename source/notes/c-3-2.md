@intro Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, obtained from crude oil. This topic covers fractional distillation, cracking, combustion and its pollutants, and free-radical substitution with chlorine.
# Fractional distillation of crude oil
- Crude oil is a mixture of mainly alkanes. It is separated by **fractional distillation**, which relies on different **boiling points**.
- The oil is heated and vaporised, then passed into a column that is **hot at the bottom and cool at the top**. Vapours rise until they reach a level cool enough to condense, and that fraction is collected there.
- Long-chain alkanes have stronger van der Waals forces, so they boil higher and are collected near the bottom. Short chains (gases such as LPG, and petrol) come off at the top.
# Cracking
:::def
**Cracking:** breaking long-chain alkanes into shorter, more useful alkanes and alkenes by breaking C–C bonds.
:::
| | Thermal cracking | Catalytic cracking |
|---|---|---|
| conditions | high temperature (up to about 1000 °C) and high pressure (up to 70 atm) | slight pressure, high temperature (about 450 °C), **zeolite** catalyst |
| main products | a high percentage of **alkenes** | **motor fuels** and **aromatic** hydrocarbons |
- **Why crack? (the economic reasons)** Fractional distillation gives more long-chain fractions than the market needs, but there is more demand for, and a higher value of, short-chain fractions such as petrol. Cracking converts low-value heavy fractions into these high-value products, and alkenes are valuable feedstocks for polymers.
- Example: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₂H₄.
# Combustion of alkanes
- **Complete combustion** happens in plenty of oxygen: C₈H₁₈ + 12½O₂ → 8CO₂ + 9H₂O. Alkanes are good fuels because they release a lot of energy.
- **Incomplete combustion** happens in limited oxygen. It produces **CO** (toxic, because it binds to haemoglobin) and **carbon (soot)**, which causes breathing problems.
- **Pollutants from engines:**
  - **Unburned hydrocarbons and NOₓ** form photochemical smog. NO forms because the high temperature in an engine lets N₂ and O₂ react.
  - **SO₂** comes from sulfur impurities and causes acid rain.
  - **CO₂** is a greenhouse gas that contributes to climate change.
- **Catalytic converters** use Pt, Pd or Rh on a ceramic honeycomb: 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂, and hydrocarbons + NO → CO₂ + N₂ + H₂O.
- **Flue gas desulfurisation** removes SO₂ from power-station emissions using CaO or CaCO₃: CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃.
:::ex Balancing a combustion equation
Q: Write equations for the complete combustion of propane and for its incomplete combustion to carbon monoxide.
1. Complete: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.
2. Incomplete: C₃H₈ + 3½O₂ → 3CO + 4H₂O.
A: Less oxygen is needed for the incomplete combustion, which forms toxic CO.
:::
# Chlorination of alkanes: free-radical substitution
In **UV light**, alkanes react with chlorine: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl.
1. **Initiation:** Cl₂ → 2Cl•. UV light breaks the Cl–Cl bond by homolytic fission.
2. **Propagation:** Cl• + CH₄ → HCl + •CH₃, then •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•. The radical is regenerated, so this is a **chain reaction**.
3. **Termination:** two radicals combine, for example Cl• + •CH₃ → CH₃Cl, or 2•CH₃ → C₂H₆, or 2Cl• → Cl₂.
- Further substitution gives CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃ and CCl₄. So the reaction gives a **mixture of products** and is not very useful for making one specific compound. An excess of methane favours CH₃Cl.
:::ex Explaining a by-product
Q: Explain why traces of ethane are found when methane reacts with chlorine.
1. •CH₃ radicals form in the propagation stage.
2. Two methyl radicals can combine in a termination step: •CH₃ + •CH₃ → C₂H₆.
A: Ethane forms from a termination step between two methyl radicals.
:::
:::warn
- Writing the propagation steps so that they produce H• radicals. The first propagation step forms HCl and •CH₃.
- Leaving out "UV light" as the condition for initiation.
- Mixing up the conditions and products of thermal and catalytic cracking.
:::
:::tip
- Learn the full chlorination mechanism, with the dots on the radicals, for any alkane. For example, ethane gives •C₂H₅.
- For pollution questions, give the pollutant, its source, its effect, and how it is removed.
:::
:::sum
- Fractional distillation separates crude oil by boiling point.
- Cracking: thermal cracking (high T and P) gives alkenes; catalytic cracking (zeolite) gives fuels and aromatics.
- Complete combustion gives CO₂ and H₂O. Incomplete combustion gives CO and C. Catalytic converters remove CO, NOₓ and hydrocarbons.
- Free-radical substitution: initiation, propagation and termination in UV light, giving a mixture of products.
:::

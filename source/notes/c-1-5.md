# Collision theory
- To react, particles must collide with energy ≥ $E_a$ and with the correct orientation.
- **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution:** the area under the curve is the total number of molecules. It starts at the origin and never touches the energy axis.
- Higher $T$: the peak moves right and lower, and many more molecules have $E\ge E_a$. This is the main reason the rate rises.
- Higher concentration or pressure: more frequent collisions.
- **Catalyst:** provides an alternative route with a lower $E_a$, and is not used up.

# Measuring rate
- Rate = change in concentration per unit time. Use the gradient of a tangent.
- Methods: gas volume (syringe), mass loss, colour change (colorimeter), clock reactions.
# Exam tips
> On M–B curves, the curve for a higher temperature has a **lower** peak, further to the right, with the same area.
- Don't say "particles collide more" for temperature. Say "more particles have energy above $E_a$".

# Born–Haber cycles
- Lattice enthalpy of formation: gaseous ions → 1 mol of solid lattice (exothermic). AQA also uses lattice dissociation, which is endothermic.
- Steps: atomisation, ionisation energy(s), electron affinity(s), lattice enthalpy, and $\Delta_fH$.
- The second electron affinity of O is **endothermic** (adding an electron to a negative ion).
- A more negative (exothermic) lattice formation enthalpy comes from smaller, more highly charged ions.
- Perfect ionic model vs experimental: a difference means **covalent character** (polarisation).
!fig c-born-haber
# Solutions
$$\Delta_{sol}H=\Delta_{LE,diss}H+\sum\Delta_{hyd}H$$
# Entropy and Gibbs
- Entropy $S$ is disorder: gas > liquid > solid. More moles of gas means $\Delta S>0$.
$$\Delta S=\sum S_{products}-\sum S_{reactants}$$
$$\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$$
- Feasible if $\Delta G\le0$. At the changeover $T=\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$. Convert $\Delta S$ from J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ to kJ.
!fig c-gibbs
# Worked example
$\Delta H=+178$ kJ mol⁻¹ and $\Delta S=+161$ J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (CaCO₃ decomposition): $T=\frac{178}{0.161}=1106$ K.
# Exam tips
> Feasible doesn't mean fast: a large $E_a$ can make a feasible reaction negligibly slow.

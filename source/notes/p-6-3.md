@spec OCR H556 6.3.1 Magnetic fields; 6.3.2 Motion of charged particles; 6.3.3 Electromagnetism
@intro Moving charges produce magnetic fields, and magnetic fields push on moving charges. This topic covers the motor effect, charged particles in magnetic fields, and electromagnetic induction, which underlies generators and transformers.
# Magnetic fields
- Magnetic fields are produced by **moving charges** (currents) and by permanent magnets. Field lines run from N to S.
- The field patterns to know are: around a long straight wire (concentric circles, using the right-hand grip rule), in a flat coil, and in a solenoid (uniform inside, like a bar magnet outside).
# Force on a current-carrying conductor
:::eq
$$F=BIL\sin\theta$$
:::
- The direction comes from **Fleming's left-hand rule**: First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current, thuMb = Motion.
:::def
**Magnetic flux density (B):** the force per unit current per unit length on a wire at right angles to the field. 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹.
:::
:::ex Motor effect
Q: A 0.20 m wire carrying 3.0 A is placed at 90° to a 0.050 T field. Find the force.
1. F = BIL = 0.050 × 3.0 × 0.20.
A: F = 0.030 N
:::
# Charged particles in magnetic fields
:::eq
$$F=BQv\qquad r=\frac{mv}{BQ}$$
:::
!fig p-bfield-circle A charged particle moves in a circle in a uniform magnetic field
- The force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so the particle moves in a **circle**, and its speed does not change: BQv = mv²/r.
- **Velocity selector:** crossed electric and magnetic fields let through, undeflected, only the particles for which EQ = BQv, so v = E/B.
:::ex Proton in a field
Q: A proton moving at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ enters a 0.10 T field at right angles. Find the radius of its path (m = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg).
1. r = mv/BQ = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2.0 × 10⁶ / (0.10 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹).
A: r = 0.21 m
:::
# Electromagnetic induction
:::def
**Magnetic flux (φ):** φ = BA cos θ, in Wb (1 Wb = 1 T m²).
**Magnetic flux linkage:** Nφ, for a coil of N turns.
**Faraday's law:** the induced e.m.f. is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
**Lenz's law:** the direction of the induced e.m.f. (or current) is such that it opposes the change that produced it.
:::
:::eq
$$\varepsilon=-\frac{\Delta(N\phi)}{\Delta t}$$
:::
- On a graph of flux linkage against time, the gradient gives the e.m.f.
- **AC generator:** a coil rotating in a field has a sinusoidal flux linkage. The e.m.f. is greatest when the plane of the coil is **parallel** to the field, because that is when the flux linkage changes fastest.
:::ex Induced e.m.f.
Q: A 200-turn coil of area 4.0 × 10⁻³ m² sits at 90° to a 0.50 T field. The field falls to zero in 0.10 s. Find the average induced e.m.f.
1. Change in flux linkage = 200 × 0.50 × 4.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.40 Wb turns.
2. ε = 0.40 / 0.10.
A: ε = 4.0 V
:::
# Transformers
:::eq
$$\frac{n_s}{n_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}=\frac{I_p}{I_s}\ \ (\text{for an ideal transformer})$$
:::
- An alternating current in the primary coil produces a changing flux in the iron core. This links the secondary coil and induces an e.m.f. in it.
- Laminating the core reduces eddy currents. Transmitting power at **high voltage** means a low current, so the I²R losses in the cables are smaller.
:::ex Step-down transformer
Q: A transformer steps 230 V down to 11.5 V. The primary has 2000 turns. How many turns are on the secondary?
1. n_s = n_p × V_s/V_p = 2000 × 11.5/230.
A: n_s = 100 turns
:::
:::warn
- Using the right hand for the motor effect. Fleming's **left**-hand rule is for motors.
- Saying that the e.m.f. is maximum when the flux linkage is maximum. It is maximum when the flux linkage is **changing** fastest.
- Thinking transformers work on d.c. They need a **changing** current.
:::
:::tip
- Lenz's law is conservation of energy. If the induced current helped the change, you would get energy for nothing.
:::
:::sum
- F = BIL sin θ (Fleming's left-hand rule); F = BQv gives circular paths with r = mv/BQ; a velocity selector passes v = E/B.
- φ = BA cos θ; ε = −Δ(Nφ)/Δt (Faraday and Lenz).
- Transformers: V_s/V_p = n_s/n_p; high-voltage transmission reduces losses.
:::

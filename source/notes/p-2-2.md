@spec OCR H556 2.2.1 Measurements and uncertainties; 2.2.2 Graphs
@intro Every measurement has an uncertainty. This topic is about expressing uncertainty, combining uncertainties through calculations, and using graphs to find gradients and intercepts with their uncertainties.
# Errors and uncertainties
:::def
**Systematic error:** a consistent shift in every reading in the same direction, caused for example by a zero error or a miscalibrated instrument. It affects **accuracy**.
**Random error:** unpredictable variation between readings. It affects **precision** and is reduced by repeating and averaging.
**Precision:** how close repeated readings are to each other.
**Accuracy:** how close a reading or result is to the true value.
**Absolute uncertainty:** the ± range of a value, e.g. 12.3 ± 0.2 cm.
**Fractional uncertainty:** absolute uncertainty ÷ value. Multiply it by 100% to get the **percentage uncertainty**.
:::
- The uncertainty of a single reading is often taken as half the resolution of an analogue scale, or ±1 in the last digit of a digital meter.
- For repeated readings, uncertainty ≈ ½ × range. Leave out anomalous readings before averaging.
# Combining uncertainties
| Operation | Rule |
|---|---|
| $y=a\pm b$ | add the **absolute** uncertainties: Δy = Δa + Δb |
| $y=ab$ or $a/b$ | add the **percentage** uncertainties |
| $y=a^n$ | percentage uncertainty in y = n × percentage uncertainty in a |
:::ex Density uncertainty
Q: A cube has side 2.00 ± 0.02 cm and mass 64.0 ± 0.5 g. Find its density and the percentage uncertainty.
1. V = 2.00³ = 8.00 cm³, so ρ = 64.0/8.00 = 8.00 g cm⁻³.
2. Percentage uncertainty in the side: 0.02/2.00 = 1%. Volume depends on side³, so its uncertainty is 3%.
3. Percentage uncertainty in mass: 0.5/64.0 = 0.8%.
4. Total: 3% + 0.8% ≈ 3.8%, which is ±0.3 g cm⁻³.
A: ρ = 8.0 ± 0.3 g cm⁻³ (3.8%).
:::
:::ex Difference of two readings
Q: Two thermometer readings are 21.5 ± 0.5 °C and 35.0 ± 0.5 °C. Give the temperature rise and its percentage uncertainty.
1. Rise = 35.0 − 21.5 = 13.5 °C.
2. For a difference, add the absolute uncertainties: ±1.0 °C.
3. Percentage uncertainty = 1.0/13.5 × 100 = 7.4%.
A: 13.5 ± 1.0 °C (7.4%). Small differences have large percentage uncertainties.
:::
# Graphs
- **Straight-line graphs:** compare with $y=mx+c$. The gradient gives one quantity and the y-intercept another.
- For an **exponential** relationship $y=Ae^{kx}$, plot ln y against x: the gradient is k and the intercept is ln A.
- For a **power law** $y=kx^n$, plot log y against log x: the gradient is n and the intercept is log k.
- The **area under a graph** can be meaningful. For example, the area under a velocity–time graph is displacement, and the area under a force–extension graph is work done.
- **Error bars** show the uncertainty of each point. The worst acceptable line passes through all the error bars, and the uncertainty in the gradient = |best gradient − worst gradient|.
:::ex Linearising data
Q: The period of a pendulum is $T=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$. Which graph gives a straight line, and how do you find g?
1. Square both sides: $T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{g}l$.
2. Plot T² on the y-axis against l on the x-axis. This gives a straight line through the origin.
A: The gradient is 4π²/g, so g = 4π² ÷ gradient.
:::
:::warn
- Adding absolute uncertainties when multiplying. For products and quotients, add the **percentages**.
- Quoting an uncertainty to more than 1–2 significant figures, or a value to more decimal places than its uncertainty.
- Including an anomalous point in the best-fit line.
:::
:::tip
- Write uncertainties to 1 s.f. (or 2 s.f. if the first digit is 1), and round the value to the same decimal place.
:::
:::sum
- Systematic errors affect accuracy; random errors affect precision.
- For sums and differences add absolute uncertainties; for products and quotients add percentages; for a power n, multiply the percentage by n.
- Linearise graphs to find constants; gradient uncertainty comes from best and worst lines.
:::

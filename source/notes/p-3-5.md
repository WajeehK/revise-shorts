@intro Newton's three laws explain why things move as they do. Momentum, and the impulse that changes it, are the tools for analysing collisions and explosions.
# Newton's laws
:::def
**First law:** an object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
**Second law:** the resultant force is equal to the rate of change of momentum: $F=\Delta p/\Delta t$. For constant mass, this gives F = ma.
**Third law:** when body A exerts a force on body B, B exerts an equal and opposite force of the same type on A.
:::
- Third-law pairs act on **different** bodies and are the same type of force. Weight and the normal contact force on a book on a table are **not** a third-law pair.
# Momentum and impulse
:::eq
$$p=mv\qquad\text{impulse}=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$
:::
- Momentum is a vector, measured in kg m s⁻¹ (or N s).
- **Impulse** is the area under a force–time graph.
- Crumple zones, airbags and helmets increase the collision time, so for the same change in momentum the **force is reduced**.
:::ex Impulse on a ball
Q: A 0.15 kg ball reaches a bat at 20 m s⁻¹ and leaves at 30 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction. The contact time is 5.0 ms. Find the average force.
1. Take the outgoing direction as positive. Δp = 0.15(30 − (−20)) = 7.5 kg m s⁻¹.
2. F = Δp/Δt = 7.5/0.0050 = 1500 N.
A: 1500 N, in the direction of the outgoing ball.
:::
# Conservation of momentum
- **The principle of conservation of momentum:** for a closed system, the total momentum before an interaction equals the total momentum after it.
!fig p-collision Momentum before and after a collision
:::def
**Perfectly elastic collision:** both momentum and **kinetic energy** are conserved.
**Inelastic collision:** momentum is conserved, but some kinetic energy is transferred to other forms, such as thermal energy and sound.
:::
- Total energy is always conserved. It is only kinetic energy that may not be.
:::ex Coupling trucks
Q: A 2000 kg truck moving at 3.0 m s⁻¹ couples with a stationary 1000 kg truck. Find their common speed and the kinetic energy lost.
1. Momentum before = 2000 × 3.0 = 6000 kg m s⁻¹, so v = 6000 / 3000 = 2.0 m s⁻¹.
2. E_k before = ½ × 2000 × 9 = 9000 J. E_k after = ½ × 3000 × 4 = 6000 J.
A: v = 2.0 m s⁻¹ and 3000 J of kinetic energy is lost. The collision is inelastic.
:::
:::ex Explosion
Q: A 60 kg skater pushes off a 40 kg skater, starting from rest. The 60 kg skater moves at 2.0 m s⁻¹. Find the velocity of the other skater.
1. The total momentum is zero before, so it is zero after: 60 × 2.0 + 40v = 0.
2. v = −3.0 m s⁻¹.
A: 3.0 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction.
:::
# Collisions in two dimensions
- Conserve momentum separately in two perpendicular directions, using components. Then combine the final components with Pythagoras to find the resultant velocity.
- Momentum before and after can also be drawn as a closed vector triangle.
- **Rockets and jets** use the same idea: the momentum given to the exhaust gases backwards equals the momentum gained by the rocket forwards. The thrust is the rate of change of the gas's momentum, F = (Δm/Δt)v.
- **Linear momentum** p = mv is a vector, so its direction matters: give one direction a positive sign and the other a negative sign. This vector nature is why momentum is conserved separately in each direction in 2D collisions.
:::warn
- Forgetting signs (directions) for velocities in momentum calculations.
- Saying that momentum is lost in an inelastic collision. It is **kinetic energy** that is lost.
- Picking weight and the normal contact force as a third-law pair.
:::
:::tip
- Draw "before" and "after" diagrams with the masses, velocities and a positive direction marked.
:::
:::sum
- Newton's laws: inertia; F = Δp/Δt; equal and opposite forces on different bodies.
- Impulse = FΔt = Δp, which is the area under the F–t graph.
- Momentum is always conserved in a closed system; kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
:::

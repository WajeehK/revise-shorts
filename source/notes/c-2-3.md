# Halogen trends
- Down the group: boiling point increases (more van der Waals), electronegativity decreases, and oxidising ability decreases.
- Appearance: F₂ pale yellow gas; Cl₂ pale green gas; Br₂ red-brown liquid; I₂ grey-black solid (purple vapour).
- Displacement: a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halide. Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (orange).
# Halide ions as reducing agents
- Reducing power increases down the group (larger ions lose an electron more easily).
- With concentrated H₂SO₄: NaCl gives HCl only. NaBr gives HBr, Br₂ and SO₂. NaI gives HI, I₂, SO₂, S and H₂S (bad-egg smell).
# Silver halide test
- Add dilute HNO₃ (to remove CO₃²⁻ and OH⁻), then AgNO₃(aq). AgCl is white and dissolves in dilute NH₃. AgBr is cream and dissolves in concentrated NH₃. AgI is yellow and is insoluble in NH₃.
# Chlorine
- Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO is disproportionation; it is used to treat water (kills bacteria). In sunlight: 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4HCl + O₂.
- Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O makes bleach (cold, dilute alkali).
# Exam tips
> Explain the risk–benefit of chlorinating water: it's toxic, and can form chlorinated hydrocarbons, but it prevents disease.

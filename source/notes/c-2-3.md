@spec AQA 7405 3.2.3.1–3.2.3.2 (Required practical 4)
@intro The halogens (F to I) are reactive non-metals, and their oxidising ability falls down the group. This topic covers their trends, displacement, how halide ions reduce sulfuric acid, silver nitrate tests, and the chemistry of chlorine in water treatment and bleach.
# Trends in physical properties
| Property | Trend down the group | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| electronegativity | decreases | atoms are larger, so the bonding pair is further from the nucleus and more shielded |
| boiling point | increases | larger molecules with more electrons have stronger van der Waals forces |
- At room temperature, F₂ is a pale yellow gas, Cl₂ a green gas, Br₂ a red-brown liquid, and I₂ a grey-black solid (it forms a purple vapour).
# Oxidising ability and displacement
- Oxidising ability **decreases** down the group: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. Larger atoms attract an extra electron less strongly.
- A halogen **displaces** a less reactive halide from solution: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂. The solution turns orange (or yellow, depending on concentration).
| Added to → | KCl(aq) | KBr(aq) | KI(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl₂(aq) | no change | orange (Br₂) | brown (I₂) |
| Br₂(aq) | no change | no change | brown (I₂) |
| I₂(aq) | no change | no change | no change |
# Reducing ability of halide ions
- Reducing ability **increases** down the group: I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻. Larger ions lose an electron more easily.
- This shows clearly in their reactions with **concentrated sulfuric acid**:
| Halide | Products | Observations | What happens to S |
|---|---|---|---|
| NaF, NaCl | HF or HCl | steamy (misty) fumes | no redox; stays +6 |
| NaBr | HBr, then Br₂ and SO₂ | steamy fumes, orange-brown vapour | reduced to +4 (SO₂) |
| NaI | HI, then I₂, SO₂, S and H₂S | fumes, purple vapour or black solid, yellow solid, smell of rotten eggs | reduced to +4, 0 and −2 |
$$2\text{HBr}+\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\rightarrow\text{Br}_2+\text{SO}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
$$8\text{HI}+\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\rightarrow4\text{I}_2+\text{H}_2\text{S}+4\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
# Testing for halide ions
1. Acidify the solution with **dilute nitric acid** to remove carbonate and hydroxide ions, which would otherwise form other precipitates.
2. Add **silver nitrate** solution: Ag⁺(aq) + X⁻(aq) → AgX(s).
3. Add **ammonia** to confirm which halide is present.
| Ion | Precipitate | Dilute NH₃ | Concentrated NH₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl⁻ | white AgCl | dissolves | dissolves |
| Br⁻ | cream AgBr | does not dissolve | dissolves |
| I⁻ | yellow AgI | does not dissolve | does not dissolve |
- Fluoride gives **no precipitate**, because AgF is soluble.
# Chlorine and chlorate(I)
- **Chlorine in water.** Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO. This is **disproportionation**: Cl goes from 0 to −1 and +1. Chloric(I) acid kills bacteria.
- **In sunlight:** 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4HCl + O₂.
- **Bleach.** With cold, dilute NaOH: Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O. Sodium chlorate(I), NaClO, is the active ingredient in bleach, and is used for cleaning and for treating water.
- **Benefits and risks of chlorinating water.** It kills disease-causing microorganisms, and it leaves a residual effect that protects water in the pipes. However, chlorine is toxic, and it can react with organic matter to form chlorinated hydrocarbons that may be carcinogenic. On balance the health benefits are judged to outweigh the risks.
:::ex Identifying a halide
Q: A solution gives a cream precipitate with acidified AgNO₃. The precipitate does not dissolve in dilute ammonia but does dissolve in concentrated ammonia. Identify the ion and write the ionic equation.
1. A cream precipitate that dissolves only in concentrated NH₃ is AgBr.
2. Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s).
A: Bromide ions, Br⁻.
:::
:::ex Oxidation states in a reaction with sulfuric acid
Q: State the change in oxidation state of sulfur when NaI reacts with concentrated H₂SO₄ to give H₂S.
1. In H₂SO₄, sulfur is +6. In H₂S, it is −2.
2. Sulfur gains 8 electrons.
A: From +6 to −2: sulfur is reduced, and the iodide ions are oxidised to I₂.
:::
:::warn
- Using HCl to acidify before the AgNO₃ test. HCl adds Cl⁻ ions and gives a false positive.
- Saying that the halogens get "more reactive" down the group. Their oxidising ability decreases, while the halide ions become better reducing agents.
- Mixing up the colours: AgCl is white, AgBr is cream, AgI is yellow.
:::
:::tip
- Learn the conc. H₂SO₄ table well. Questions often ask for an observation and the name of the reduction product of sulfur for each halide.
- For chlorine and water, state one benefit and one risk, and name the disproportionation.
:::
:::sum
- Down Group 7: electronegativity and oxidising ability decrease, and the boiling point increases.
- A halogen displaces the halides below it.
- The reducing power of halides increases down the group. I⁻ reduces conc. H₂SO₄ as far as H₂S.
- AgNO₃ test: AgCl is white, AgBr cream, AgI yellow. Confirm with ammonia.
- Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO disinfects water. Cl₂ + 2NaOH gives bleach.
:::

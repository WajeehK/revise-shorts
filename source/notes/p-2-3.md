@spec OCR H556 2.3.1 Scalars and vectors
@intro Some quantities have only a size; others also have a direction. Handling directions properly, by adding and resolving vectors, underlies all of mechanics.
# Scalars and vectors
:::def
**Scalar:** a quantity with magnitude only, e.g. mass, distance, speed, time, energy, temperature.
**Vector:** a quantity with magnitude **and direction**, e.g. displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum.
:::
# Adding vectors
- **Triangle of vectors:** draw the vectors **tip to tail**. The resultant goes from the start of the first vector to the end of the last.
- For two **perpendicular** vectors, use Pythagoras to find the magnitude and trigonometry to find the angle.
$$R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\qquad \theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$
- For non-perpendicular vectors, draw an accurate **scale diagram**, or use the cosine rule.
:::ex Perpendicular forces
Q: Forces of 6.0 N east and 8.0 N north act on a body. Find the resultant.
1. R = √(6.0² + 8.0²) = √100 = 10 N.
2. θ = tan⁻¹(8.0/6.0) = 53° north of east.
A: 10 N at 53° north of east (a bearing of 037°).
:::
# Non-perpendicular vectors and vector subtraction
- For two vectors at angle θ to each other, the **cosine rule** gives the magnitude of the resultant directly.
- To **subtract** a vector, add its negative: Δv = v − u = v + (−u). Reverse the arrow for u, then add tip to tail.
:::ex Cosine rule
Q: A plane flies at 200 m s⁻¹ due north. A wind of 50 m s⁻¹ blows towards the north-east. Find the resultant speed.
1. The angle between the two vectors is 45°, so the angle inside the vector triangle is 180° − 45° = 135°.
2. R² = 200² + 50² − 2(200)(50) cos 135° = 40000 + 2500 + 14142 = 56642.
A: R ≈ 238 m s⁻¹.
:::
:::ex Change in velocity
Q: A ball hits a wall at 8.0 m s⁻¹ and rebounds along the same line at 6.0 m s⁻¹. Find the change in velocity.
1. Take towards the wall as positive. Then u = +8.0 m s⁻¹ and v = −6.0 m s⁻¹.
2. Δv = v − u = −6.0 − 8.0 = −14 m s⁻¹.
A: 14 m s⁻¹ directed away from the wall. The change in speed is only 2 m s⁻¹, but the change in velocity is 14 m s⁻¹.
:::
# Resolving vectors
- Any vector F can be split into two **perpendicular components**: F cos θ along the direction at angle θ to F, and F sin θ perpendicular to it.
- On a slope at angle θ, the weight resolves into mg sin θ **down the slope** and mg cos θ **perpendicular to the slope**.
:::eq
$$F_x=F\cos\theta,\qquad F_y=F\sin\theta$$
:::
:::ex Resolving on a slope
Q: A 5.0 kg block rests on a frictionless 30° slope. Find the components of its weight (g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹).
1. W = 5.0 × 9.81 = 49.1 N.
2. Down the slope: 49.1 sin 30° = 24.5 N.
3. Perpendicular to the slope: 49.1 cos 30° = 42.5 N, balanced by the normal contact force.
A: 24.5 N down the slope and 42.5 N into the slope.
:::
# Equilibrium and closed triangles
- If three coplanar forces keep a body in **equilibrium**, the vectors drawn tip to tail form a **closed triangle**, because the resultant is zero.
- Alternatively, resolve the forces in two perpendicular directions and set each sum to zero.
:::ex Hanging sign
Q: A 20 N sign hangs from two cords, each at 30° to the horizontal. Find the tension in each cord.
1. By symmetry, both tensions are equal, T.
2. Vertically: 2T sin 30° = 20, so T = 20 N.
A: Each tension is 20 N.
:::
:::warn
- Adding vector magnitudes directly, e.g. claiming 6 N + 8 N = 14 N whatever the directions.
- Swapping sin and cos. The component **adjacent** to the angle uses cos.
- Giving a vector answer without a direction.
:::
:::tip
- Always draw a sketch with the angle marked before resolving.
:::
:::sum
- Scalars have magnitude only; vectors have magnitude and direction.
- Add vectors tip to tail, using Pythagoras or a scale drawing.
- Resolve into components F cos θ and F sin θ.
- In equilibrium the forces form a closed triangle.
:::

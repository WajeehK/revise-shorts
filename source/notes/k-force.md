@intro Newton's laws link forces to motion. This topic covers force diagrams, F = ma (with vectors and on slopes), connected particles and pulleys, and friction.
# Newton's laws
:::def
**First law:** an object remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force. Zero resultant force means **equilibrium**.
**Second law:** $F=ma$, where $F$ is the **resultant** force.
**Third law:** if body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.
:::
- Forces to show on a force diagram: weight $mg$, normal reaction $R$, tension $T$ (or thrust in a rod), friction $F$, and any driving or resistance forces.
- **Vector form:** $\mathbf F=m\mathbf a$. Add the force vectors, then divide by $m$.
:::ex Vector forces
Q: Forces $(3\mathbf i+2\mathbf j)$ N and $(1\mathbf i-5\mathbf j)$ N act on a 2 kg particle. Find its acceleration and the magnitude of the acceleration.
1. Resultant $=4\mathbf i-3\mathbf j$, so $\mathbf a=2\mathbf i-1.5\mathbf j$.
A: $\mathbf a=(2\mathbf i-1.5\mathbf j)$ m s⁻², with magnitude $2.5$ m s⁻².
:::
## Equilibrium problems
- In equilibrium, resolve in two perpendicular directions and set each resultant to zero. Choose directions that make the algebra easy, often along and perpendicular to an unknown force.
- With three forces, you can also use a **triangle of forces** and the sine rule (Lami's theorem is not required).
# Inclined planes
!fig k-incline Forces on a particle on an inclined plane
- Resolve **parallel** and **perpendicular** to the slope. The weight has components $mg\sin\theta$ down the slope and $mg\cos\theta$ into the slope, so $R=mg\cos\theta$ (with no other perpendicular forces).
# Friction (A level)
- $F\le\mu R$. The friction force takes whatever value is needed, up to a maximum of $\mu R$.
- **Limiting equilibrium** (on the point of moving) and **moving**: $F=\mu R$. Friction always opposes motion, or the tendency to move.
:::ex Rough slope
Q: A 4 kg block slides down a rough 30° slope with $\mu=0.2$. Find its acceleration.
1. $R=4g\cos30°=33.9$ N, so $F=0.2\times33.9=6.79$ N.
2. Down the slope: $4g\sin30°-6.79=4a$, so $19.6-6.79=4a$.
A: $a=3.20$ m s⁻²
:::
# Connected particles and pulleys
!fig k-pulley Two particles connected over a smooth pulley
- Connected by a **light inextensible** string: the tension is the same on both sides of a smooth pulley, and both particles have the same magnitude of acceleration.
- Write $F=ma$ for each particle separately, then solve simultaneously. Alternatively, treat the whole system as one body, but only if it moves in a straight line.
:::ex Pulley system
Q: Masses of 5 kg and 3 kg hang either side of a smooth pulley. Find the acceleration and the tension.
1. 5 kg particle: $5g-T=5a$. 3 kg particle: $T-3g=3a$.
2. Adding: $2g=8a$, so $a=\frac g4=2.45$ m s⁻².
3. $T=3g+3a=29.4+7.35$.
A: $a=2.45$ m s⁻² and $T=36.75$ N (36.8 N)
:::
:::ex Towing a trailer
Q: A 1200 kg car tows a 400 kg trailer with a light rigid towbar. The driving force is 2400 N, and the resistances are 300 N on the car and 100 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the tension in the towbar.
1. Whole system: $2400-400=1600a$, so $a=1.25$ m s⁻².
2. Trailer: $T-100=400\times1.25$, so $T=600$ N.
A: $a=1.25$ m s⁻² and $T=600$ N
:::
- **Force on the pulley:** the two tensions combine to give a resultant force on the pulley. For vertical strings it is $2T$.
- **The value of g.** Gravitational acceleration g is taken as 9.8 m s⁻² (SI units), or 9.81 or 10 if a question says so. Give answers to 2 or 3 s.f. when g = 9.8 is used. g is assumed constant near the Earth's surface.
- **The coefficient of friction** μ depends on the two surfaces in contact. A rough surface has μ > 0; a smooth surface means μ = 0 (no friction).
:::warn
- Writing $R=mg$ on a slope, or when another force has a vertical component.
- Using $F=\mu R$ when the object is not moving and not on the point of moving.
- Treating a pulley system as one body without thinking about which way each force acts.
:::
:::tip
- Draw a large, clear force diagram for **each** particle, with the direction of the acceleration marked by a double arrow.
:::
:::sum
- Equilibrium means zero resultant force. $\mathbf F=m\mathbf a$, including vector form.
- On slopes, resolve parallel and perpendicular to the slope.
- $F\le\mu R$, with equality when limiting or moving.
- Connected particles: same tension and same acceleration; write F = ma for each.
:::

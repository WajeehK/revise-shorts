@intro Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon compounds. Before studying any reactions you need the language: how to name compounds, the different kinds of formula, how mechanisms are drawn, and the types of isomerism.
# Types of formula
| Formula | What it shows | Example (butan-1-ol) |
|---|---|---|
| empirical | simplest whole-number ratio of atoms | C₄H₁₀O |
| molecular | actual number of atoms | C₄H₁₀O |
| general | algebraic formula for a homologous series | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| structural | arrangement of atoms with no bonds drawn | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH |
| displayed | every atom and every bond | all C–H, C–C, C–O and O–H bonds drawn |
| skeletal | carbon skeleton as lines; C and H on carbons not shown | a zig-zag of 4 carbons with OH at the end |
:::def
**Homologous series:** a family of compounds with the same functional group and general formula, in which each member differs from the next by CH₂.
**Functional group:** the group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.
:::
# Naming (IUPAC nomenclature)
1. Find the **longest carbon chain** containing the functional group. Its length gives the stem: meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-.
2. Add the suffix for the main functional group: -ane, -ene, -ol, -al, -one, -oic acid, -nitrile.
3. Number the chain to give the functional group, then any substituents, the **lowest numbers**.
4. Put prefixes for substituents in **alphabetical order**: bromo-, chloro-, methyl-. Use di- or tri- for repeats.
| Series | Suffix or prefix | Example |
|---|---|---|
| alkene | -ene | but-2-ene |
| alcohol | -ol | propan-2-ol |
| halogenoalkane | halo- prefix | 2-chloro-2-methylpropane |
| aldehyde | -al | ethanal |
| ketone | -one | butanone |
| carboxylic acid | -oic acid | propanoic acid |
:::ex Naming a branched compound
Q: Name CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(Br)CH₃.
1. The longest chain has 4 carbons, so the stem is butane.
2. Number from the end that gives the substituents the lowest numbers: Br on C2 and CH₃ on C3.
3. Put the prefixes in alphabetical order: bromo before methyl.
A: 2-bromo-3-methylbutane
:::
# Reaction mechanisms
- **Curly arrows** show the movement of a **pair of electrons**. An arrow starts from a lone pair or a bond, and ends at an atom or at the space where a new bond forms.
- **Heterolytic fission:** both bonding electrons go to one atom, forming ions. **Homolytic fission:** one electron goes to each atom, forming **radicals** (species with an unpaired electron, written with a dot, for example Cl•).
- A **nucleophile** is an electron-pair donor, for example OH⁻, CN⁻ or NH₃. An **electrophile** is an electron-pair acceptor, for example H⁺, NO₂⁺ or the δ+ end of Br₂.
- **Free-radical** reactions are written with stages: initiation, propagation and termination.
# Isomerism
:::def
**Structural isomers:** compounds with the same molecular formula but a different structural formula.
**Stereoisomers:** compounds with the same structural formula but a different arrangement of atoms in space.
:::
- **Chain isomers** have different carbon skeletons, for example butane and methylpropane.
- **Position isomers** have the functional group in different positions, for example propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol.
- **Functional group isomers** have different functional groups, for example propanal and propanone, or ethanol and methoxymethane.
- **E/Z isomerism** happens because rotation about a C=C bond is **restricted**, and **each carbon of the C=C has two different groups** attached.
- **CIP priority rules:** on each carbon of the C=C, the attached atom with the higher atomic number has the higher priority. If the higher-priority groups are on the **same side**, the isomer is **Z** (zusammen); if they are on **opposite sides**, it is **E** (entgegen).
:::ex E/Z naming
Q: Is CH₃CH=CHCl with CH₃ and Cl on opposite sides of the double bond the E or Z isomer?
1. Left-hand carbon: CH₃ (C, atomic number 6) beats H, so CH₃ has priority.
2. Right-hand carbon: Cl (atomic number 17) beats H, so Cl has priority.
3. The two priority groups are on opposite sides.
A: E-1-chloroprop-1-ene
:::
:::ex Counting structural isomers
Q: Draw or name the structural isomers of C₄H₉Br.
1. Straight chain: 1-bromobutane and 2-bromobutane.
2. Branched chain: 1-bromo-2-methylpropane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
A: There are four structural isomers. (2-bromobutane also has optical isomers.)
:::
:::warn
- Numbering a chain from the wrong end, or not listing substituent prefixes alphabetically.
- Starting a curly arrow from an atom instead of from a lone pair or a bond.
- Saying that any alkene shows E/Z isomerism. Each C of the C=C must carry two **different** groups, so but-1-ene does not.
:::
:::tip
- In mechanism questions, marks are given for each curly arrow, each lone pair and each partial charge. Draw lone pairs on nucleophiles.
- When you assign E/Z, write the atomic numbers on the diagram to show your working.
:::
:::sum
- Formula types: empirical, molecular, general, structural, displayed and skeletal.
- IUPAC: longest chain, lowest numbers, alphabetical prefixes.
- Curly arrows show electron pairs; heterolytic fission gives ions and homolytic fission gives radicals.
- Isomerism: structural (chain, position, functional group) or stereo (E/Z from restricted rotation, assigned using CIP priorities).
:::

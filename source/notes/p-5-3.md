@intro Pendulums, masses on springs and vibrating molecules all oscillate. Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is the model behind them all, and resonance explains why bridges sway and why microwave ovens heat food.
# Defining SHM
:::def
**Simple harmonic motion:** oscillation in which the acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position and is directed towards that position: $a=-\omega^2x$.
:::
- The terms (amplitude, period, frequency, angular frequency ω = 2πf, and phase difference) are the same as for waves.
- The **period of SHM is independent of the amplitude**. This is why a pendulum is such a good timer (for small angles).
!fig p-shm Displacement, velocity and acceleration against time in SHM
:::eq
$$x=A\cos\omega t\ \ (\text{or }A\sin\omega t)\qquad v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\qquad v_\text{max}=\omega A$$
:::
- Velocity is the gradient of the x–t graph, and it is ¼ cycle (90°) ahead of displacement. Acceleration is in antiphase with displacement.
:::ex Mass on a spring
Q: A mass oscillates with SHM, with amplitude 0.050 m and frequency 2.0 Hz. Find the maximum speed, the maximum acceleration, and the speed at x = 0.030 m.
1. ω = 2π × 2.0 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹, so v_max = 12.6 × 0.050 = 0.63 m s⁻¹.
2. a_max = ω²A = 158 × 0.050 = 7.9 m s⁻².
3. v = 12.6 × √(0.050² − 0.030²) = 12.6 × 0.040 = 0.50 m s⁻¹.
A: 0.63 m s⁻¹, 7.9 m s⁻² and 0.50 m s⁻¹
:::
# Energy in SHM
- Energy is continually exchanged between kinetic and potential energy, but the total stays constant (if there is no damping).
- The kinetic energy is maximum at x = 0. The potential energy is maximum at x = ±A.
- Total energy is proportional to A²: $E=\tfrac12m\omega^2A^2$.
:::ex Energy of an oscillator
Q: A 0.20 kg mass oscillates with ω = 10 rad s⁻¹ and A = 0.040 m. Find the total energy.
1. E = ½ × 0.20 × 100 × 0.040².
A: E = 0.016 J
:::
# Damping, forced oscillations and resonance
:::def
**Damping:** a loss of energy to the surroundings (usually by friction), which reduces the amplitude.
**Free oscillation:** an oscillation at the natural frequency with no driving force.
**Forced oscillation:** an oscillation driven by a periodic external force at the driving frequency.
**Resonance:** a maximum amplitude, which occurs when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.
:::
- **Light damping:** the amplitude decays gradually, often exponentially. **Critical damping:** the system returns to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillating (as in car suspension). **Heavy damping:** a slow return with no oscillation.
- More damping **lowers and broadens** the resonance peak, and shifts it slightly to a lower frequency.
- Examples of resonance: useful in musical instruments, radio tuning and microwave ovens; harmful in bridges (the Millennium Bridge) and machinery.
:::prac PAG 10: investigating SHM
- Time 10 or more oscillations of a mass on a spring, starting the timer as the mass passes the **equilibrium position**, using a fiducial marker.
- For a spring, T = 2π√(m/k), so plot T² against m to find k. For a pendulum, T = 2π√(l/g).
- Use a motion sensor or a data logger to plot displacement–time graphs and observe damping.
:::
- **Solutions to a = −ω²x.** The displacement is x = A cos ωt if the oscillator starts at maximum displacement, or x = A sin ωt if it starts at the equilibrium position. Both solutions have the same period, T = 2π/ω.
:::warn
- Forgetting the minus sign in a = −ω²x. It shows that the acceleration is directed towards the centre.
- Saying that resonance happens at "any high frequency". It happens at the **natural** frequency.
- Mixing up sin and cos. Use cos if timing starts at maximum displacement, and sin if it starts at equilibrium.
:::
:::tip
- Write ω = 2πf first. Most SHM calculations need ω.
:::
:::sum
- SHM: a = −ω²x; the period is independent of the amplitude.
- x = A cos ωt; v_max = ωA; v = ±ω√(A² − x²).
- KE and PE interchange; E ∝ A².
- Damping types and resonance at the natural frequency.
:::

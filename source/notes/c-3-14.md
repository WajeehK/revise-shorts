@intro Organic synthesis means planning a sequence of reactions that turns a starting material into a target molecule. You need to know every reaction in the course: its reagents, its conditions and the functional group change it makes.
# Principles of a good synthesis
- Use as **few steps** as possible, because every step loses some yield.
- Choose reactions with a **high atom economy** and a high percentage yield.
- **Avoid hazardous solvents** and starting materials where possible, and reduce waste. These are principles of green chemistry.
- Look at how the **carbon chain length** changes. Reactions with KCN (on halogenoalkanes or carbonyls) and Friedel–Crafts acylation add carbon atoms.
# Key aliphatic conversions
| From | To | Reagents and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| alkane | halogenoalkane | Cl₂ or Br₂, UV light |
| alkene | halogenoalkane | HBr, room temperature |
| alkene | alcohol | steam, H₃PO₄, 300 °C, 6–7 MPa |
| halogenoalkane | alcohol | NaOH(aq), warm |
| halogenoalkane | alkene | KOH in ethanol, hot |
| halogenoalkane | nitrile | KCN in ethanol/water, reflux |
| halogenoalkane | amine | excess NH₃ in ethanol, heat under pressure |
| nitrile | amine | LiAlH₄, or H₂/Ni |
| primary alcohol | aldehyde | K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, distil |
| primary alcohol or aldehyde | carboxylic acid | K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, reflux |
| secondary alcohol | ketone | K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, reflux |
| aldehyde or ketone | alcohol | NaBH₄ (aqueous) |
| aldehyde or ketone | hydroxynitrile | KCN, then dilute acid |
| alcohol | alkene | conc. H₃PO₄ or H₂SO₄, heat |
| carboxylic acid + alcohol | ester | conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, warm |
| acyl chloride | ester, amide or acid | alcohol, NH₃/amine, or water at room temperature |

|---|---|---|
| benzene | nitrobenzene | conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 50 °C |

# Planning a route
1. Draw the starting material and the target. Compare their functional groups and count the carbons.
2. Work **backwards** from the target: "Which functional group could make this?"
3. Write each step with its intermediate, its reagents and its **conditions**.
:::ex Two-step synthesis
Q: Plan a synthesis of propanoic acid from 1-bromopropane.
1. Both have 3 carbons, so no carbon needs to be added.
2. Step 1: NaOH(aq), warm (nucleophilic substitution), gives propan-1-ol.
3. Step 2: acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, excess, heated under **reflux**, gives propanoic acid.
A: 1-bromopropane → propan-1-ol → propanoic acid
:::
:::ex Extending the chain
Q: Convert bromoethane into propylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
1. The target has 3 carbons and the starting material has 2, so a carbon must be added. Use cyanide.
2. Step 1: KCN in ethanol, reflux, gives propanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CN.
3. Step 2: reduce with LiAlH₄ (or H₂/Ni), giving CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
A: bromoethane → propanenitrile → propylamine
:::
:::ex Aromatic route
Q: Give a two-step route from benzene to 1-phenylethanol, C₆H₅CH(OH)CH₃.
1. Step 1: Friedel–Crafts acylation with CH₃COCl and AlCl₃ gives phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃.
2. Step 2: reduce the ketone with NaBH₄, giving the secondary alcohol (as a racemate).
A: benzene → phenylethanone → 1-phenylethanol
:::
:::warn
- Leaving out conditions such as "reflux", "aqueous" or "in ethanol". These often carry the mark. For example, KOH in **ethanol** gives elimination, but in **water** it gives substitution.
- Forgetting to add a carbon when the target is longer than the starting material.
- Suggesting a direct one-step route that does not exist, for example alkane to alcohol.
:::
:::tip
- Make a reaction map on one sheet of paper, with functional groups as the nodes and reagents on the arrows. Revise from it.
- For every step, name the **type** of reaction too (e.g. nucleophilic substitution), since questions often ask for it.
:::
:::sum
- Plan backwards from the target and keep the number of steps low.
- Know every reagent and condition. KCN (and Friedel–Crafts acylation) adds carbons.
- Choose safe, high-atom-economy routes and avoid hazardous solvents.
:::

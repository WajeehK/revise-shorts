@spec AQA 7405 3.3.5.1–3.3.5.3 (Required practical 5)
@intro Alcohols contain the –OH group. This topic covers how ethanol is made (fermentation and hydration), the debate over biofuels, the oxidation of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols, and dehydration to alkenes.
# Classifying alcohols
| Type | Carbon bonded to OH has | Example |
|---|---|---|
| primary (1°) | one alkyl group | propan-1-ol |
| secondary (2°) | two alkyl groups | propan-2-ol |
| tertiary (3°) | three alkyl groups | 2-methylpropan-2-ol |
# Production of ethanol
**1. Fermentation of glucose**
$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\rightarrow2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}+2\text{CO}_2$$
- Conditions: **yeast** enzymes, **anaerobic**, about **35 °C**, in aqueous solution. The ethanol is separated by fractional distillation.
- The temperature is a compromise. It must be warm enough for a reasonable rate, but not so hot that the enzymes are denatured.
- **Advantages:** it uses renewable sugar crops and needs little energy. **Disadvantages:** it is slow, it is a batch process, the ethanol is impure, and it uses farmland.
**2. Hydration of ethene**
$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_4(\text{g})+\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})\rightleftharpoons\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}(\text{g})$$
- Conditions: **phosphoric acid catalyst**, 300 °C, 6–7 MPa. It is fast, continuous and gives pure ethanol, but it uses non-renewable crude oil.
# Biofuels and carbon neutrality
:::def
**Carbon neutral:** an activity with no net annual emission of carbon dioxide to the atmosphere.
:::
- Bioethanol is sometimes called carbon neutral. Photosynthesis takes in 6CO₂, then fermentation releases 2CO₂ and combustion releases 4CO₂, so these cancel.
- In practice it is **not fully carbon neutral**, because fossil fuels are burned for farming, transport, making fertiliser and distillation.
# Oxidation of alcohols
The oxidising agent is **acidified potassium dichromate(VI)**, K₂Cr₂O₇ with H₂SO₄. It changes from **orange to green** as Cr₂O₇²⁻ is reduced to Cr³⁺. The oxygen from the oxidising agent is written [O].
| Alcohol | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| primary | **distil immediately**, so the aldehyde is removed as it forms | **aldehyde** |
| primary | **heat under reflux** with excess oxidising agent | **carboxylic acid** |
| secondary | heat under reflux | **ketone** |
| tertiary | — | **not oxidised**; the solution stays orange |
:::eq
$$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}+[\text{O}]\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}+\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
$$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}+2[\text{O}]\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}+\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
$$\text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CH}_3+[\text{O}]\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3+\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
:::
- **Distinguishing an aldehyde from a ketone.** **Tollens' reagent** ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺), warmed, gives a **silver mirror** with an aldehyde. **Fehling's solution** (or Benedict's), warmed, changes from blue to a **brick-red precipitate** (Cu₂O) with an aldehyde. Ketones give no change in either test.
:::ex Predicting oxidation products
Q: Butan-2-ol is heated under reflux with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇. Name the product, state the colour change, and write the equation.
1. Butan-2-ol is a secondary alcohol, so it is oxidised to a ketone.
2. The dichromate is reduced from orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ to green Cr³⁺.
3. CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₂CH₃ + H₂O.
A: Butanone; orange to green.
:::
# Dehydration (elimination)
- Heating an alcohol with **concentrated H₃PO₄ or H₂SO₄** (acid catalyst) eliminates water to form an **alkene**: CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O.
- **Mechanism:** the OH is protonated by H⁺ and leaves as H₂O, giving a carbocation. Then an H⁺ is lost from the adjacent carbon to form the C=C.
- This gives a route to polymers from renewable sources (bio-ethanol to ethene to poly(ethene)) without using crude oil.
- Unsymmetrical alcohols can give a mixture of alkenes, including E/Z isomers. For example, butan-2-ol gives but-1-ene and E- and Z-but-2-ene.
:::ex Products of dehydration
Q: Give the structures of all the alkenes formed when pentan-2-ol is dehydrated.
1. The H can be lost from C1 or from C3.
2. Loss from C1 gives pent-1-ene. Loss from C3 gives pent-2-ene, which has E and Z forms.
A: pent-1-ene, E-pent-2-ene and Z-pent-2-ene
:::
:::prac Required practical 5: distillation of a product
- To prepare an **aldehyde**, add acidified dichromate dropwise to warm ethanol and distil immediately. Keep the receiver cool in ice; ethanal boils at 21 °C.
- To prepare a **carboxylic acid**, heat under **reflux**. The vapours condense and drip back, so the volatile aldehyde intermediate is fully oxidised. Then distil off the product.
- Add anti-bumping granules and heat with a water bath or an electric heater. Never seal the apparatus.
:::
:::warn
- Saying that tertiary alcohols are oxidised "to ketones". They are **not** oxidised, because there is no H on the C–OH carbon.
- Mixing up distillation (to collect the aldehyde) with reflux (to make the acid).
- Saying that bioethanol is fully carbon neutral without mentioning the energy used in production.
:::
:::tip
- Use [O] in oxidation equations and balance with H₂O. Examiners accept this for organic oxidation.
- In "compare the two methods" questions, cover rate, purity, the type of process, the raw materials (renewable or not) and the energy needed.
:::
:::sum
- Ethanol is made by fermentation (yeast, 35 °C, anaerobic) or by hydration of ethene (H₃PO₄, 300 °C, 6–7 MPa).
- Bioethanol is not fully carbon neutral in practice.
- Oxidation with H⁺/Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange to green): primary gives an aldehyde (distil) or a carboxylic acid (reflux); secondary gives a ketone; tertiary has no reaction.
- Tollens' and Fehling's tests identify aldehydes. Dehydration with a concentrated acid gives alkenes.
:::

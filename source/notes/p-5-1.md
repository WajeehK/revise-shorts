@intro Thermal physics links the large-scale properties we measure (temperature, pressure, volume) to the random motion of particles. It covers heating and changes of state, and the ideal gas model.
# Temperature and the kinetic model
- **Thermal equilibrium:** there is no net flow of thermal energy between objects at the same temperature.
- **Absolute zero** (0 K = −273 °C) is the lowest possible temperature, at which particles have minimum internal energy. T(K) = θ(°C) + 273.
- **Kinetic model:** solids have particles vibrating about fixed positions; liquids have particles close together but able to move past each other; gases have particles far apart, moving fast and randomly.
- **Brownian motion** (for example, smoke particles seen jiggling under a microscope) is evidence for the random motion of the much smaller, unseen air molecules colliding with them.
:::def
**Internal energy:** the sum of the randomly distributed kinetic and potential energies of the particles in a system.
:::
- During a **change of state**, the potential energy changes but the kinetic energy (and therefore the temperature) stays constant.
!fig p-heating Heating curve: the temperature is flat during changes of state
# Specific heat capacity and latent heat
:::eq
$$E=mc\Delta\theta\qquad E=mL$$
:::
- **Specific heat capacity, c:** the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K (in J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹).
- **Specific latent heat, L:** the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature. L_f is for fusion (melting) and L_v for vaporisation.
:::ex Ice to water
Q: How much energy is needed to turn 0.20 kg of ice at 0 °C into water at 20 °C? (L_f = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹; c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹)
1. Melting: 0.20 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 66 800 J.
2. Warming: 0.20 × 4200 × 20 = 16 800 J.
A: Total = 83 600 J ≈ 8.4 × 10⁴ J
:::
:::prac PAG 8: specific heat capacity using the electrical method
- Heat an insulated metal block with an immersion heater. Measure V, I and t, and the rise in temperature.
- Then c = VIt / (mΔθ). Insulating the block reduces heat loss, which would otherwise make c appear too large.
:::
# Ideal gases
:::eq
$$pV=nRT\qquad pV=NkT\qquad pV=\tfrac13Nm\overline{c^2}$$
:::
- R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, k = R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
- **Boyle's law:** pV = constant at constant T. Pressure is also ∝ T at constant volume.
- **Assumptions of the kinetic theory:** a large number of molecules moving randomly; elastic collisions; molecular volume negligible compared with the container; no intermolecular forces except during collisions; collision time negligible.
- **Mean kinetic energy** of a molecule: $\tfrac12m\overline{c^2}=\tfrac32kT$. The internal energy of an ideal gas is all kinetic and is proportional to T.
- **Root-mean-square speed:** $c_\text{rms}=\sqrt{\overline{c^2}}$.
:::ex Gas in a cylinder
Q: 0.50 mol of gas is held at 300 K in a 0.010 m³ container. Find the pressure, and the mean KE of a molecule.
1. p = nRT/V = 0.50 × 8.31 × 300 / 0.010 = 1.25 × 10⁵ Pa.
2. Mean KE = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J.
A: p = 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa (to 2 s.f.) and mean KE = 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J
:::
:::prac PAG 7: estimating absolute zero, and Boyle's law
- **Absolute zero:** heat a flask of air in a water bath and record the pressure at several temperatures. Extrapolate the p–θ line back to p = 0. The intercept is about −273 °C.
- **Boyle's law:** compress a trapped column of air with a pump and read p and V. Wait between readings so that the temperature stays constant. Plot p against 1/V to get a straight line.
:::
- **Temperature scales.** Temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (°C) and in kelvin (K): T/K = θ/°C + 273. A change of 1 °C is the same size as a change of 1 K.
- **Internal energy.** Heating a body increases its internal energy; during a change of state this goes into potential energy, not kinetic energy, so the temperature is constant.
- **Amount of substance.** Amount of substance is measured in moles. One mole contains the Avogadro constant N_A of particles, which equals 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. So N = n × N_A.
:::warn
- Using °C in the gas equations. Always use **kelvin**.
- Saying that the temperature rises during melting.
- Confusing n (moles) with N (number of molecules).
:::
:::tip
- Choose pV = nRT when you are given moles, and pV = NkT when you are given molecules.
:::
:::sum
- Internal energy = random KE + PE of the particles; temperature is flat during changes of state.
- E = mcΔθ and E = mL.
- pV = nRT = NkT = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩; mean KE = (3/2)kT.
:::

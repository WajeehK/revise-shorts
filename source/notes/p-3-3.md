@spec OCR H556 3.3.1 Work and conservation of energy; 3.3.2 Kinetic and potential energies; 3.3.3 Power
@intro Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred. Work is how a force transfers energy, and power is how quickly the transfer happens.
# Work done
:::def
**Work done:** force × distance moved in the direction of the force. 1 J is the work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in the direction of the force.
:::
$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$
!fig p-work-angle Only the component of the force along the motion does work
:::ex Pulling a sledge
Q: A rope pulls a sledge 20 m with a force of 50 N at 30° above the horizontal. Find the work done.
1. Only the horizontal component does work: 50 cos 30° = 43.3 N.
2. W = 43.3 × 20 = 866 J.
A: About 870 J.
:::
# Conservation of energy
- The **principle of conservation of energy**: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another. The total energy of a closed system stays constant.
- Energy stores include kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical and nuclear.
# Kinetic and gravitational potential energy
:::eq
$$E_k=\tfrac12mv^2\qquad E_p=mgh$$
:::
- **Derivation of E_k.** A force F accelerates a mass m from rest over a distance x. Then W = Fx = ma·x, and v² = 2ax gives ax = v²/2. So W = ½mv².
- **Derivation of E_p.** Lifting mass m at a steady speed through height h needs a force mg, so the work done is mgh.
- For a falling object with no air resistance: mgh = ½mv², so $v=\sqrt{2gh}$, which is independent of the mass.
:::ex Roller coaster
Q: A cart starts from rest 20 m above the lowest point of a track. Find its speed at the bottom (ignore friction).
1. mgh = ½mv², and the mass cancels.
2. v = √(2 × 9.81 × 20) = √392.4 = 19.8 m s⁻¹.
A: About 20 m s⁻¹.
:::
:::ex Energy lost to friction
Q: A 2.0 kg box slides down a 3.0 m high slope and reaches the bottom at 6.0 m s⁻¹. How much energy is transferred to thermal energy?
1. Loss of E_p = 2.0 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 58.9 J.
2. Gain of E_k = ½ × 2.0 × 6.0² = 36 J.
A: 58.9 − 36 = 22.9 J is transferred to thermal energy by friction.
:::
# Work done against resistive forces
- When an object moves at **constant velocity**, the driving force does work against friction or drag at the same rate. The energy goes into thermal energy, so the kinetic energy does not change.
- Work done by a variable force = the area under its force–displacement graph.
# Power and efficiency
:::def
**Power:** the rate of doing work (or of transferring energy), $P=W/t$. 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹.
**Efficiency:** useful output energy ÷ total input energy × 100%.
:::
- For a force moving at a constant velocity: $P=Fv$, because P = Fx/t = Fv.
:::ex Car power
Q: A car moves at a steady 30 m s⁻¹ against a total resistive force of 600 N. Its engine is 25% efficient. Find the input power.
1. Useful power = Fv = 600 × 30 = 18 kW.
2. Input power = 18 / 0.25 = 72 kW.
A: 72 kW.
:::
:::warn
- Using W = Fx when the force is at an angle to the motion. Include cos θ.
- Using a vertical distance when the motion is along a slope (or the other way round). For E_p, h is always the **vertical** height.
- Giving an efficiency greater than 100%.
:::
:::tip
- In energy questions, state "loss of E_p = gain of E_k (+ work done against friction)" before substituting.
:::
:::sum
- W = Fx cos θ; energy is conserved.
- E_k = ½mv² and E_p = mgh; both can be derived from W = Fx.
- P = W/t = Fv; efficiency = useful output ÷ total input.
:::

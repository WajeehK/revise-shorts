# Bonding types
- **Ionic:** electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in a giant lattice. High melting point; conducts when molten or dissolved.
- **Covalent:** a shared pair of electrons. **Dative:** both electrons come from one atom (e.g. NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺).
- **Metallic:** a lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons.
- Giant covalent: diamond (4 bonds, hard), graphite (3 bonds, delocalised electrons, conducts, layers slide), SiO₂.
# Shapes (VSEPR)
- Pairs: 2 linear 180°; 3 trigonal planar 120°; 4 tetrahedral 109.5°; 5 trigonal bipyramidal 90°/120°; 6 octahedral 90°.
- Each lone pair reduces the angle by about 2.5°: NH₃ is 107° (pyramidal), H₂O is 104.5° (bent).
- Lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs.

# Polarity and intermolecular forces
- Electronegativity: the power of an atom to attract the pair of electrons in a covalent bond (F is highest).
- Van der Waals (induced dipole) forces < permanent dipole–dipole forces < hydrogen bonds (N, O or F bonded to H, attracted to a lone pair on N, O or F).
- Ice is less dense than water because hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in an open lattice.

# Exam tips
> A symmetrical molecule with polar bonds (e.g. CO₂, CCl₄) is **non-polar** because the dipoles cancel.
- When asked to explain a boiling point, name the force **and** say it is weak or strong relative to the others and needs more or less energy to overcome.

# Carboxylic acids and esters
- Weak acids: RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺. With a carbonate they produce CO₂.
- Esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst). Esters are used in perfumes, flavourings, plasticisers and solvents.
- Hydrolysis: acid hydrolysis is reversible; alkaline hydrolysis goes to completion, giving a carboxylate salt.
- Fats and oils are triglycerides. NaOH hydrolysis gives soap plus glycerol. With methanol (KOH catalyst) they give biodiesel.
# Acyl chlorides and anhydrides
- Nucleophilic addition–elimination with water (forms an acid), an alcohol (ester), NH₃ (amide) or an amine (N-substituted amide). HCl is also formed.
- Anhydrides react the same way but more slowly and produce RCOOH instead of HCl. They are used industrially (e.g. for aspirin): cheaper and less corrosive.
- Mechanism: the lone pair attacks the δ+ C, the C=O π bond breaks to O⁻, then C=O reforms, Cl⁻ leaves, and H⁺ is lost.
# Exam tips
> Name esters with the alcohol part first: CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ is ethyl ethanoate.

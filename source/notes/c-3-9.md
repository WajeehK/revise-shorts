@intro Carboxylic acids contain –COOH. Their derivatives (esters, acyl chlorides, acid anhydrides and amides) are made from them and turn up in flavours, fats, soaps, biodiesel and medicines such as aspirin.
# Carboxylic acids
- Carboxylic acids are **weak acids** that partly dissociate: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺.
- They react with **carbonates** and hydrogencarbonates to release CO₂, which is the test for an acid: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂.
- They also react with metals (giving H₂) and with bases (giving a salt and water).
# Esters
- **Esterification:** carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, using a concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst and warming. For example, CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O, which gives ethyl ethanoate.
- **Naming:** the alcohol part comes first ("ethyl"), then the acid part ("ethanoate").
- **Uses:** solvents, **plasticisers** for polymers, and perfumes and food **flavourings**, because many esters are sweet-smelling.
- **Hydrolysis.** Acid hydrolysis (dilute acid, heat under reflux) is reversible and gives the acid and the alcohol. Base hydrolysis (NaOH, reflux) goes to completion and gives the **carboxylate salt** and the alcohol.
# Fats, soaps and biodiesel
- Vegetable oils and animal fats are **esters of propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol)** with three long-chain fatty acids, known as triglycerides.
- Hydrolysis with hot NaOH gives glycerol and the **sodium salts of the fatty acids**, which are **soaps**.
- **Biodiesel** is a mixture of **methyl esters** of long-chain fatty acids. It is made by reacting vegetable oils with **methanol** and a strong-alkali catalyst (KOH).
:::ex Products of ester hydrolysis
Q: Name the products when methyl propanoate is heated under reflux with NaOH(aq).
1. The ester is CH₃CH₂COOCH₃. The alcohol part is methyl and the acid part is propanoate.
2. Alkaline hydrolysis gives the salt of the acid plus the alcohol.
A: Sodium propanoate, CH₃CH₂COONa, and methanol.
:::
# Acylation: acyl chlorides and acid anhydrides
- **Acyl chlorides** (RCOCl, e.g. ethanoyl chloride) and **acid anhydrides** ((RCO)₂O) are acylating agents.
| Reacts with | Product from RCOCl (+ HCl) | Product from (RCO)₂O (+ RCOOH) |
|---|---|---|
| water | carboxylic acid | carboxylic acid |
| alcohol | ester | ester |
| ammonia | primary amide | primary amide |
| primary amine | N-substituted amide | N-substituted amide |
- With ammonia and amines, the HCl reacts with excess base to form an ammonium salt.
- **Mechanism (nucleophilic addition–elimination), for acyl chlorides:**
1. The lone pair on the nucleophile (H₂O, ROH, NH₃ or RNH₂) attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon, and the C=O π electrons move to O.
2. The C=O re-forms and **Cl⁻ is eliminated**.
3. Loss of H⁺ (taken by Cl⁻) gives the product and HCl.
- **Ethanoic anhydride** is used industrially to make **aspirin**. It is cheaper than ethanoyl chloride, less corrosive, reacts less vigorously with water, and does not produce corrosive HCl fumes.
:::ex Acyl chloride with an amine
Q: Give the product and equation for ethanoyl chloride with methylamine.
1. Methylamine is a primary amine, so the product is an N-substituted amide.
2. CH₃COCl + CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + HCl. With excess amine, the HCl forms CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻.
A: N-methylethanamide.
:::
:::prac Required practical 10: preparing an organic solid and an organic liquid
- **Solid (e.g. aspirin):** react, collect the crude solid by **vacuum filtration** with a Büchner funnel, and then **recrystallise**. Dissolve the crude product in the **minimum volume of hot solvent**, filter hot to remove insoluble impurities, cool to crystallise, filter, wash with a little cold solvent, and dry.
- **Testing purity:** a pure solid has a **sharp melting point** that matches the data-book value. Impurities lower the melting point and make it melt over a range.
- **Liquid (e.g. an ester):** distil, then shake with NaHCO₃(aq) in a **separating funnel** to remove acid, releasing the pressure often. Run off the aqueous layer, dry with anhydrous CaCl₂ or MgSO₄, and redistil, collecting the fraction at the boiling point.
:::
:::warn
- Writing the ester name the wrong way round. The **alcohol** part comes first.
- Forgetting HCl as the second product of acyl chloride reactions, or forgetting that an anhydride gives a carboxylic acid instead.
- Using "excess hot solvent" in recrystallisation. It must be the **minimum**, or the product stays dissolved.
:::
:::tip
- The addition–elimination mechanism scores marks for: the lone pair and arrow to C; the arrow from C=O to O; the tetrahedral intermediate; C=O re-forming with Cl⁻ leaving; and the loss of H⁺.
:::
:::sum
- Carboxylic acids are weak acids; they fizz with carbonates.
- Esters come from acid + alcohol with an H₂SO₄ catalyst. They are hydrolysed by acid (reversible) or by NaOH (giving the salt).
- Fats with NaOH give soaps and glycerol; with methanol and KOH they give biodiesel.
- RCOCl and (RCO)₂O acylate water, alcohols, NH₃ and amines by addition–elimination.
:::

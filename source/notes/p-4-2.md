@intro This topic is about energy in circuits: the difference between e.m.f. and p.d., resistance and the I–V characteristics of components, resistivity, and electrical power.
# E.m.f. and potential difference
:::def
**Potential difference (p.d.):** the energy transferred **from** electrical energy to other forms, per unit charge: $V=W/Q$.
**Electromotive force (e.m.f.):** the energy transferred **to** electrical energy from other forms (for example, chemical energy in a cell), per unit charge.
**Volt:** 1 V = 1 J C⁻¹.
**Electronvolt:** the energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V. 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
:::
- The energy transferred to an electron accelerated through a p.d. V is $eV=\tfrac12mv^2$.
:::ex Accelerated electron
Q: An electron is accelerated from rest through 500 V. Find its speed (mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg).
1. Energy gained = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 500 = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ J.
2. v = √(2E/m) = √(2 × 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹).
A: v = 1.3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹
:::
# Resistance and I–V characteristics
- **Resistance**, R = V/I. 1 Ω = 1 V A⁻¹.
- **Ohm's law:** for a metallic conductor at constant temperature, the current is directly proportional to the p.d.
!fig p-iv I–V characteristics of a resistor, a filament lamp and a diode
| Component | I–V characteristic |
|---|---|
| fixed resistor | straight line through the origin (ohmic) |
| filament lamp | a curve that flattens as I increases. The filament heats up, the ions vibrate more, collisions with electrons increase, and R rises |
| diode / LED | almost no current in reverse; conducts above a **threshold p.d.** (about 0.6 V for silicon, more for LEDs) |
| NTC thermistor | resistance **falls** as temperature rises, because n increases |
| LDR | resistance **falls** as light intensity rises |
# Resistivity
:::eq
$$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$$
:::
- **Resistivity**, ρ (in Ω m), is a property of the material and depends on temperature. For metals, ρ increases with temperature. For semiconductors, ρ decreases.
:::prac PAG 3: determining resistivity
- Measure the diameter of a wire with a micrometer at several places, and work out A.
- Connect lengths L of wire (using crocodile clips) and measure V and I to find R. Plot R against L: the gradient is ρ/A.
- Keep the current small, or switch off between readings, so that the wire does not heat up and change its resistance.
:::
:::ex Resistivity
Q: A 2.0 m nichrome wire has a diameter of 0.40 mm and a resistance of 17.5 Ω. Find ρ.
1. A = π(0.20 × 10⁻³)² = 1.26 × 10⁻⁷ m².
2. ρ = RA/L = 17.5 × 1.26 × 10⁻⁷ / 2.0.
A: ρ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m
:::
# Electrical power and energy
:::eq
$$P=VI=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}\qquad W=VIt$$
:::
- The **kilowatt-hour (kW h)** is the energy transferred by a 1 kW device in 1 hour: 1 kW h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
- Cost = energy used (in kW h) × the price per kW h.
:::ex Cost of a kettle
Q: A 2.5 kW kettle runs for 4.0 minutes a day for 30 days at 28p per kW h. Find the cost.
1. Energy = 2.5 kW × (4/60 h) × 30 = 5.0 kW h.
2. Cost = 5.0 × 28p.
A: £1.40
:::
:::warn
- Mixing up e.m.f. (energy given **to** charges) and p.d. (energy taken **from** charges).
- Using the radius as the diameter in A = πr².
- Saying that a filament lamp's resistance rises "because current increases". It rises because the **temperature** increases.
:::
:::tip
- Choose the power equation that uses the quantity that is the same for every component: I²R in series, V²/R in parallel.
:::
:::sum
- V = W/Q; e.m.f. is energy transferred to electrical energy per unit charge; 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
- Learn the I–V shapes for a resistor, lamp, diode, thermistor and LDR.
- R = ρL/A. P = VI = I²R = V²/R, and 1 kW h = 3.6 MJ.
:::

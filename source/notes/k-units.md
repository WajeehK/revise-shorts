@spec Edexcel 9MA0 Mechanics topic 6: 6.1 S.I. units and derived quantities; modelling assumptions and vocabulary
@intro Mechanics models real objects with simplified mathematical versions of them. This topic covers the units used in mechanics and, just as importantly, the modelling vocabulary (particle, light, smooth, inextensible) that each assumption brings.
# S.I. units
| Quantity | Unit | In base units |
|---|---|---|
| length or displacement | metre (m) | m |
| time | second (s) | s |
| mass | kilogram (kg) | kg |
| velocity | m s⁻¹ | m s⁻¹ |
| acceleration | m s⁻² | m s⁻² |
| force or weight | newton (N) | kg m s⁻² |
- 1 N is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m s⁻².
- **Weight** is $W=mg$. The mass stays the same everywhere, but the weight depends on $g$. Take $g=9.8$ m s⁻² unless told otherwise.
- Using $g=9.8$ means giving answers to 2 or 3 significant figures. Never give more accuracy than the data justifies.
:::ex Converting units
Q: Convert 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
1. $72\times1000=72000$ m per hour.
2. $72000\div3600=20$.
A: $20$ m s⁻¹
:::
:::ex Weight and mass
Q: A box has a mass of 12 kg. Find its weight on Earth, and on the Moon, where $g=1.6$ m s⁻².
1. Earth: $12\times9.8=117.6$ N.
2. Moon: $12\times1.6=19.2$ N.
A: 118 N on Earth and 19.2 N on the Moon. The mass is 12 kg in both places.
:::
# Modelling assumptions
| Term | Meaning | Effect on the model |
|---|---|---|
| **particle** | the object's dimensions are negligible | its mass acts at a single point; ignore rotation and air resistance |
| **rod** | all dimensions except one are negligible | it is rigid and does not bend |
| **lamina** | an object with area but negligible thickness | — |
| **uniform** | the mass is evenly distributed | its centre of mass is at the geometric centre |
| **light** | the object has negligible mass | the tension is the same throughout a light string |
| **inextensible** | the string does not stretch | connected objects have the same acceleration |
| **smooth** | there is no friction | the only contact force is the normal reaction |
| **rough** | there is friction | friction opposes the motion |
| **wire** | a rigid, thin length of metal | treated as one-dimensional |
| **bead** | a particle with a hole through it, threaded on a wire | it moves freely along the wire |
| **peg** | a support from which an object can hang or rest | it is often modelled as smooth |
| **air resistance** | resistance to motion through the air | usually ignored for particles |
| **gravity** | the force of attraction towards the Earth | assumed to be uniform, with $g=9.8$ m s⁻² |
# Making and criticising models
1. Simplify the real problem with assumptions.
2. Solve the mathematical model.
3. Compare the predictions with reality. Refine the model, for example by including air resistance or treating the object as a rod instead of a particle.
:::ex Effect of an assumption
Q: A ball thrown upwards is modelled as a particle with no air resistance. State one effect of each assumption.
1. Treating it as a particle means that its size can be ignored, so it has no rotation or spin.
2. Ignoring air resistance means that the only force is its weight, so its acceleration is a constant $g$ downwards.
A: The model will overestimate the maximum height, because real air resistance slows the ball.
:::
:::warn
- Calling weight "mass", or giving weight in kg. Weight is a force, measured in **N**.
- Forgetting to convert km h⁻¹ or g into S.I. units.
- Listing an assumption without saying how it affects the model, when the question asks for that.
:::
:::tip
- "State a modelling assumption" questions want a word from the table **and** its consequence in context.
:::
:::sum
- S.I. units: m, s, kg; derived units: m s⁻¹, m s⁻², N.
- $W=mg$, with $g=9.8$ m s⁻².
- Learn the modelling vocabulary and what each assumption means for the model.
:::

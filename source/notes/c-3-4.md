# Alkenes
- C=C is a σ bond plus a π bond. The π electrons are a region of high electron density, so alkenes attract electrophiles.
- Bromine water changes from orange to colourless.
# Electrophilic addition
- HBr gives a bromoalkane. Br₂ gives a dibromoalkane (Br₂ is polarised by the C=C). Cold concentrated H₂SO₄ gives an alkyl hydrogensulfate, which then forms an alcohol with water.
- Mechanism: π electrons attack the electrophile, forming a carbocation intermediate; then Br⁻ attacks.
- **Markovnikov:** the major product comes from the more stable carbocation (tertiary > secondary > primary). Alkyl groups have a positive inductive effect.

# Addition polymers
- Poly(ethene), poly(propene) and PVC are made from alkenes. Repeat unit: –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.
- They are unreactive (strong non-polar C–C and C–H bonds), so they are not biodegradable. Plasticisers make PVC flexible.
# Exam tips
> For an unsymmetrical alkene, draw the carbocation and say **why** it is the more stable one.

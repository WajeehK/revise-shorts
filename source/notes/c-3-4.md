@intro Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons with a C=C double bond. The electron-rich double bond makes them react by electrophilic addition. This topic also covers addition polymers.
# Structure, bonding and reactivity
- General formula CₙH₂ₙ for alkenes with one C=C.
- The C=C is made of a σ bond and a **π bond**. The π bond is formed by sideways overlap of p orbitals, above and below the plane of the molecule.
- The π bond is a region of **high electron density**, so it attracts **electrophiles**. It is weaker than the σ bond, which is why alkenes are more reactive than alkanes.
- Rotation about the C=C is restricted, which gives E/Z isomerism.
# Electrophilic addition
!fig c-elec-add Electrophilic addition of HBr to an alkene
| Reagent | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| HBr | room temperature | bromoalkane |
| Br₂ | room temperature, in an organic solvent or as bromine water | dibromoalkane |
| conc. H₂SO₄, then water | cold acid, then warm with water | alcohol (H₂SO₄ is a catalyst overall) |
**Mechanism with HBr:**
1. The π electrons attack the δ+ H of H–Br. The H–Br bond breaks heterolytically, with both electrons going to Br.
2. This forms a **carbocation** and a Br⁻ ion.
3. The lone pair on Br⁻ attacks the positive carbon.
**With Br₂:** the bromine molecule is non-polar, but as it approaches the electron-rich C=C its electrons are repelled, inducing a dipole (Br$^{\delta+}$–Br$^{\delta-}$). After that the mechanism is the same as for HBr.
- **Test for unsaturation.** Shake with **bromine water**. It changes from **orange to colourless** with an alkene. With an alkane it stays orange.
# Unsymmetrical alkenes: major and minor products
- Adding HBr to propene can form two carbocations. The **secondary carbocation**, CH₃C⁺HCH₃, is **more stable** than the primary one, CH₃CH₂C⁺H₂.
- Stability order: **tertiary > secondary > primary**. Alkyl groups have a positive inductive effect: they push electrons towards the positive carbon and spread out its charge.
- The more stable carbocation forms faster, so **2-bromopropane is the major product** and 1-bromopropane the minor one.
:::ex Major product
Q: Name the major product of the reaction of 2-methylpropene with HBr, and explain why it forms.
1. The H can add to either carbon of the C=C. If it adds to the CH₂ end, a tertiary carbocation, (CH₃)₃C⁺, forms.
2. A tertiary carbocation is the most stable, because three alkyl groups donate electron density.
A: 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, formed through the more stable tertiary carbocation.
:::
:::ex Addition of sulfuric acid
Q: Ethene reacts with cold concentrated H₂SO₄, and the product is warmed with water. Name the intermediate and the final product.
1. Electrophilic addition of H₂SO₄ gives ethyl hydrogensulfate, CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH.
2. Hydrolysis with water gives ethanol and re-forms H₂SO₄.
A: Ethyl hydrogensulfate, then ethanol.
:::
# Addition polymers
- Alkenes join together through their C=C bonds to make **addition polymers**. Poly(ethene) is made from ethene, and poly(propene) from propene.
- To draw a **repeating unit**, open the C=C, put the substituents on the two carbons, and add extension bonds through square brackets with n.
:::ex Drawing a repeating unit
Q: Draw the repeating unit of poly(chloroethene) (PVC).
1. The monomer is CH₂=CHCl.
2. Open the double bond and keep the Cl on one carbon.
A: –[CH₂–CHCl]ₙ–
:::
- **Properties.** Poly(alkenes) are saturated, with only strong C–C and C–H bonds, so they are **unreactive** and **not biodegradable**.
- **PVC** is rigid. Adding a **plasticiser** pushes the polymer chains apart, weakening the forces between them, so the chains slide and the material becomes flexible. Rigid PVC is used for window frames and pipes; flexible PVC for cable insulation.
:::warn
- Drawing the first curly arrow from the H of HBr to the C=C. It starts **from the double bond** and goes to the H.
- Saying that the major product forms because "the Br goes to the carbon with fewer H". Explain using **carbocation stability**.
- Drawing a polymer repeating unit with a C=C still in it.
:::
:::tip
- In the Br₂ mechanism, show the induced dipole on Br₂ and the curly arrow from the Br–Br bond to Br$^{\delta-}$.
- For "explain the major product", draw both carbocations, label them primary, secondary or tertiary, and state their relative stability.
:::
:::sum
- The C=C is a σ bond plus a π bond. The π bond is electron-rich and attacks electrophiles.
- Electrophilic addition of HBr, Br₂ and H₂SO₄ goes through a carbocation. Bromine water decolourises.
- Carbocation stability is tertiary > secondary > primary, which decides the major product.
- Addition polymers are unreactive. Plasticisers make PVC flexible.
:::

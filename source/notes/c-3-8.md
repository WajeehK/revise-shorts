@intro Aldehydes and ketones both contain the carbonyl group, C=O. Aldehydes can be oxidised further and ketones cannot, which is the basis of the tests that tell them apart. Both undergo nucleophilic addition.
# Structure and naming
- **Aldehydes** have the C=O at the end of the chain, as –CHO. Examples are ethanal (CH₃CHO) and propanal.
- **Ketones** have the C=O within the chain, as R–CO–R′. Examples are propanone (CH₃COCH₃) and butanone.
- The C=O is **polar**, C$^{\delta+}$=O$^{\delta-}$, and **planar**. The δ+ carbon is attacked by nucleophiles.
# Oxidation and tests
- Aldehydes are oxidised to carboxylic acids by acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange to green): RCHO + [O] → RCOOH. Ketones are not oxidised.
| Test | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|
| Tollens' reagent, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, warm | **silver mirror** (Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag) | no change |
| Fehling's solution (blue Cu²⁺ complex), warm | **brick-red precipitate** of Cu₂O | stays blue |
| acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, warm | orange → green | stays orange |
# Reduction with NaBH₄
- **NaBH₄** in aqueous or alcoholic solution reduces aldehydes to **primary alcohols** and ketones to **secondary alcohols**. The reducing agent is written [H].
$$\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}+2[\text{H}]\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$$
$$\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3+2[\text{H}]\rightarrow\text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CH}_3$$
- **Mechanism (nucleophilic addition).** The hydride ion, H⁻, from NaBH₄ attacks the δ+ carbon, and the C=O π electrons move onto O, forming an alkoxide ion. The O⁻ then takes H⁺ from water, giving –OH.

# Addition of cyanide: making hydroxynitriles
- Carbonyl compounds react with **KCN followed by dilute acid** (or HCN) to form **hydroxynitriles**. For example, propanone gives 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CN. This adds a carbon to the chain.
- **Mechanism:** the lone pair on CN⁻ attacks the δ+ carbon, the C=O π electrons move to O to give O⁻, and the O⁻ is protonated by H⁺ (or HCN).
- **Hazards.** KCN is highly toxic if swallowed or absorbed, and HCN is a toxic gas. KCN is used in preference to HCN because it is a solid, which is less likely to be inhaled, and it gives a higher concentration of the CN⁻ nucleophile. Work in a fume cupboard.
- With aldehydes and unsymmetrical ketones the product is **chiral**. Because the C=O is planar, attack from either side gives a **racemic mixture**.
:::ex Naming a hydroxynitrile
Q: Name the product of ethanal with KCN and dilute acid, and say whether it is optically active.
1. CH₃CHO + HCN → CH₃CH(OH)CN.
2. The longest chain, including the CN carbon, has 3 carbons. The OH is on C2: 2-hydroxypropanenitrile.
3. C2 has four different groups (H, OH, CH₃, CN), so it is chiral. But the planar C=O is attacked equally from both sides, so a racemate forms.
A: 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, formed as a racemic mixture, so it is not optically active.
:::
:::ex Distinguishing isomers
Q: Describe a test to distinguish propanal from propanone, two isomers of formula C₃H₆O.
1. Warm each with Tollens' reagent in a water bath.
2. Propanal (an aldehyde) is oxidised, so Ag⁺ is reduced to silver.
A: Propanal gives a silver mirror; propanone shows no change.
:::
:::ex Reduction product
Q: Name the product when butanone is reduced with NaBH₄, and classify it.
1. Butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃, is a ketone, so reduction gives a secondary alcohol.
2. The OH ends up on C2.
A: Butan-2-ol, a secondary alcohol (with a chiral C2, formed as a racemate).
:::
:::warn
- In the NaBH₄ mechanism, showing H⁺ attacking first. The nucleophile is **H⁻**.
- In the HCN mechanism, starting the arrow from the C of CN⁻ incorrectly. It starts from the **lone pair on carbon**.
- Saying that Fehling's solution gives a silver mirror.
:::
:::tip
- Nucleophilic addition marks: the δ+ and δ− on C=O, the lone pair on the nucleophile, an arrow to C, an arrow from the C=O bond to O, the intermediate, then protonation.
:::
:::sum
- Aldehydes are oxidised (Tollens' gives a silver mirror; Fehling's gives a brick-red precipitate); ketones are not.
- NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes to primary alcohols and ketones to secondary alcohols, by nucleophilic addition of H⁻.
- KCN with acid gives hydroxynitriles by nucleophilic addition of CN⁻, often as racemates. KCN is highly toxic.
:::

# Aldehydes and ketones
- Both contain C=O. Aldehydes: RCHO. Ketones: RCOR'.
- Aldehydes are oxidised (Tollens' gives a silver mirror; Fehling's turns from blue to a brick-red Cu₂O precipitate). Ketones are not.
# Nucleophilic addition
- NaBH₄ (reduction, [H]): aldehyde → primary alcohol; ketone → secondary alcohol. The nucleophile is H⁻.
- KCN, then dilute acid: gives a hydroxynitrile, adding one C. The nucleophile is CN⁻, and the product is racemic if chiral.
- Mechanism: the nucleophile attacks the δ+ C of C=O, the π bond breaks onto O⁻, then O⁻ is protonated.

# Reduction and tests in detail
- Reduction with NaBH₄ in aqueous ethanol: ethanal + 2[H] → ethanol; propanone + 2[H] → propan-2-ol.
- Tollens' reagent ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺) is reduced to Ag; Fehling's (Cu²⁺, blue) is reduced to Cu₂O (red). The aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylic acid (or its salt in alkali).
- Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ also oxidises aldehydes (orange → green) but not ketones.
# Worked example
Ethanal + KCN, then acid: CN⁻ attacks the δ+ carbon, giving CH₃CH(OH)CN, 2-hydroxypropanenitrile. The C=O was planar, so attack from either face is equally likely and the product is a racemic mixture.
# Common mistakes
- Saying ketones give a positive Tollens' test: they do not.
- Drawing the curly arrow from the O instead of from the C=O bond, or from the nucleophile's negative charge instead of its lone pair.
# Exam tips
> KCN is used instead of HCN because HCN is a toxic gas, but KCN is toxic too. Work in a fume cupboard.

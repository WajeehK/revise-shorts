@intro Light sometimes behaves as particles, called photons, and particles such as electrons sometimes behave as waves. This topic covers the evidence: the photoelectric effect and electron diffraction.
# Photons
:::def
**Photon:** a quantum (a discrete packet) of electromagnetic energy.
:::
:::eq
$$E=hf=\frac{hc}{\lambda}\qquad h=6.63\times10^{-34}\text{ J s}$$
:::
- To convert from J to eV, divide by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹. Visible photons have energies of about 2–3 eV.
:::ex Photon energy
Q: Find the energy, in J and in eV, of a photon of wavelength 500 nm.
1. E = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ = 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
2. In eV: 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.49 eV.
A: 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, or 2.49 eV
:::
:::prac PAG 6: estimating h using LEDs
- Each LED emits light of a known wavelength when the p.d. across it reaches the **threshold p.d.** V₀. At that point, the energy given to one electron, eV₀, roughly equals the photon energy hc/λ.
- Find V₀ for several LEDs, for example by increasing V until the LED just lights (viewed through a tube to block other light), or from the I–V graph.
- Plot V₀ against 1/λ. The gradient is hc/e, so h = gradient × e/c.
:::
# The photoelectric effect
!fig p-photoelectric The photoelectric effect: photons release electrons from a metal surface
- When EM radiation of a high enough frequency falls on a metal, **electrons are emitted** (photoelectrons). This can be shown with a charged gold-leaf electroscope: UV light makes the leaf of a negatively charged zinc plate fall.
**Key observations, which the wave model cannot explain:**
1. There is a **threshold frequency** f₀ below which no electrons are emitted, however intense the light.
2. Emission is **instantaneous**.
3. The **maximum kinetic energy** of the photoelectrons depends on the frequency, not on the intensity.
4. A higher intensity (above f₀) gives **more** electrons per second, but not faster ones.
- **Explanation.** One photon interacts with **one electron**, in a one-to-one interaction. The electron needs at least the **work function** φ to escape the surface.
:::eq
$$hf=\phi+KE_\text{max}\qquad \phi=hf_0$$
:::
- A graph of KE_max against f is a straight line with gradient h. The x-intercept is f₀, and the y-intercept is −φ.
:::ex Photoelectric equation
Q: UV light of frequency 1.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz falls on zinc (φ = 4.3 eV). Find the maximum KE of the photoelectrons.
1. hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 1.5 × 10¹⁵ = 9.95 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 6.2 eV.
2. KE_max = 6.22 − 4.3 = 1.9 eV, which is 1.92 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.1 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
A: 1.9 eV (3.1 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)
:::
# Wave–particle duality
- **Electron diffraction:** a beam of electrons passing through thin graphite forms rings on a fluorescent screen. Diffraction is a wave property, so this is evidence that particles behave as waves.
:::eq
$$\lambda=\frac{h}{p}=\frac{h}{mv}\qquad(\text{de Broglie})$$
:::
- Diffraction is noticeable when λ is similar to the atomic spacing (about 10⁻¹⁰ m).
:::ex de Broglie wavelength
Q: Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 4.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
1. p = mv = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 4.0 × 10⁶ = 3.64 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹.
2. λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / 3.64 × 10⁻²⁴.
A: λ = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ m, similar to atomic spacings, so it would diffract.
:::
- Energy levels of electrons in atoms, and line spectra, follow from photons. They are covered in topic 5.5, but they use the same E = hf.
!fig p-energy-levels Electron energy levels and photon emission
- **Einstein's photoelectric equation** is hf = φ + KEmax, where KEmax is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons.
:::prac Estimating the Planck constant with LEDs
Use LEDs of different colours. For each, find the threshold p.d. V at which it just starts to light, and the wavelength λ of its light. At threshold eV ≈ hc/λ, so plot V against 1/λ: the gradient is hc/e, and the Planck constant is h = gradient × e/c.
:::
:::warn
- Saying that brighter light gives "faster" electrons. It gives **more** electrons.
- Forgetting the eV to J conversion before using hf = φ + KE.
- Saying that photoelectrons "absorb several photons". It is one photon per electron.
:::
:::tip
- In explanations, use the key phrases: one-to-one interaction; energy is conserved; the work function is the minimum energy needed to free an electron.
:::
:::sum
- E = hf = hc/λ; LEDs can be used to estimate h.
- Photoelectric effect: hf = φ + KE_max; there is a threshold frequency; intensity affects only the number of electrons.
- Electron diffraction shows that particles have wavelength λ = h/mv.
:::

@intro How do chemists find out what an unknown organic compound is? This topic covers simple test-tube tests for functional groups, high-resolution mass spectrometry for molecular formulae, and infrared spectroscopy for identifying bonds.
# Test-tube tests for functional groups
| Functional group | Test | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| alkene (C=C) | shake with bromine water | orange → colourless |
| primary or secondary alcohol | warm with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | orange → green |
| tertiary alcohol | warm with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | stays orange |
| aldehyde | warm with Tollens' reagent | silver mirror |
| aldehyde | warm with Fehling's solution | blue solution → brick-red precipitate |
| carboxylic acid | add NaHCO₃ or Na₂CO₃ | effervescence; the CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| halogenoalkane | warm with NaOH(aq), acidify with HNO₃, then add AgNO₃ | white, cream or yellow precipitate |
:::prac Required practical 6: identifying functional groups
- Use small volumes in test tubes and warm in a water bath, never over a flame, because many organic compounds are flammable. Tollens' reagent must be freshly prepared.
- Do the tests in a logical order. For example, test with NaHCO₃ for an acid first, then Tollens' for an aldehyde, then dichromate to distinguish primary/secondary alcohols from tertiary ones.
:::
# High-resolution mass spectrometry
- High-resolution mass spectrometry measures $m/z$ to **4 or 5 decimal places**. This distinguishes compounds that have the same integer $M_r$ but different molecular formulae.
- Uses precise atomic masses: ¹H = 1.0078, ¹²C = 12.0000, ¹⁶O = 15.9949, ¹⁴N = 14.0031.
:::ex Choosing a molecular formula
Q: A compound has a molecular ion at $m/z$ = 44.0262. Is it CO₂, C₃H₈ or C₂H₄O?
1. CO₂ = 12.0000 + 2(15.9949) = 43.9898.
2. C₃H₈ = 3(12.0000) + 8(1.0078) = 44.0624.
3. C₂H₄O = 24.0000 + 4.0312 + 15.9949 = 44.0261.
A: C₂H₄O, whose precise mass matches 44.0262.
:::
# Infrared spectroscopy
- Covalent bonds **absorb IR radiation** at characteristic frequencies, which makes them vibrate more (stretch or bend). An IR spectrum plots % transmittance against **wavenumber** (cm⁻¹); absorptions appear as dips.
| Bond | Wavenumber (cm⁻¹) | Appearance |
|---|---|---|
| O–H (alcohols) | 3230–3550 | broad |
| O–H (carboxylic acids) | 2500–3000 | very broad |
| N–H (amines) | 3300–3500 | medium |
| C–H | 2850–3300 | sharp |
| C=O | 1680–1750 | strong, sharp |
| C=C | 1620–1680 | medium |
| C–O | 1000–1300 | strong |
- The **fingerprint region** (below about 1500 cm⁻¹) is unique to each compound. Comparing it with a database spectrum identifies the compound exactly. It also shows impurities, which give extra peaks.
- **Greenhouse gases.** CO₂, CH₄ and H₂O absorb IR radiation emitted by the Earth's surface, which leads to global warming.
:::ex Interpreting an IR spectrum
Q: A compound C₃H₆O₂ shows a very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and a strong peak at 1715 cm⁻¹. Identify it.
1. A very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ is the O–H of a carboxylic acid.
2. The peak at 1715 cm⁻¹ is a C=O.
3. Three carbons with COOH gives propanoic acid.
A: Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH
:::
:::ex Following a reaction by IR
Q: How could IR show that propan-1-ol has been fully oxidised to propanal?
1. Propan-1-ol has a broad O–H absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹.
2. Propanal has a C=O absorption at about 1720 cm⁻¹ and no O–H.
A: The broad O–H peak disappears and a strong C=O peak appears.
:::
:::warn
- Mixing up the two O–H ranges: 3230–3550 cm⁻¹ for alcohols and 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ for acids.
- Trying to read individual peaks in the fingerprint region. You compare its overall pattern with known spectra.
- Saying that Fehling's solution gives a "silver mirror". That is Tollens' reagent; Fehling's gives a brick-red precipitate.
:::
:::tip
- The data booklet gives the IR table. Quote the bond **and** its wavenumber range in your answer.
- In combined-technique questions, use the molecular ion for $M_r$, IR for the functional groups, and the tests for confirmation.
:::
:::sum
- Tests: bromine water for C=C, dichromate for 1°/2° alcohols, Tollens' or Fehling's for aldehydes, NaHCO₃ for acids.
- High-resolution MS gives precise masses, which identify the molecular formula.
- IR: bonds absorb at characteristic wavenumbers. The fingerprint region identifies the compound. Greenhouse gases absorb IR.
:::

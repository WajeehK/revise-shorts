@intro The Periodic Table is arranged so that patterns repeat across each period. This topic covers the s, p, d and f blocks and the trends in atomic radius, first ionisation energy and melting point across Period 3 (Na to Ar).
# Classification into blocks
- Elements are arranged in order of **atomic (proton) number**, in periods (rows) and groups (columns).
- An element's **block** is the sub-shell that holds its highest-energy electron:
| Block | Groups | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| s | 1 and 2 (plus H and He) | Na, Mg |
| p | 13–18 | Al to Ar |
| d | transition metals | Sc to Zn |
| f | lanthanides and actinides | Ce, U |
:::def
**Periodicity:** the repeating pattern of physical and chemical properties across periods.
:::
# Atomic radius across Period 3
- Atomic radius **decreases** from Na to Ar.
- Across the period the **nuclear charge increases** (one more proton each time), while the extra electrons go into the **same shell**, so the **shielding is similar**.
- The outer electrons are therefore pulled closer to the nucleus.
# First ionisation energy across Period 3
- The general trend is an **increase** from Na to Ar, for the same reason: more nuclear charge, similar shielding, a smaller radius, so the outer electron is held more strongly.
- **Al is lower than Mg.** Al's outer electron is in a **3p** sub-shell, which is slightly higher in energy and is shielded by the 3s electrons.
- **S is lower than P.** In S, two electrons share one 3p orbital. The **repulsion between the paired electrons** makes one of them easier to remove.

# Melting points across Period 3
| Element | Structure | Bonding or force overcome | Melting point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na, Mg, Al | metallic lattice | metallic bonding, which gets stronger from Na to Al | rises Na < Mg < Al |
| Si | macromolecular (giant covalent) | many strong covalent bonds | highest |
| P₄, S₈, Cl₂ | simple molecular | weak van der Waals forces | low; S₈ > P₄ > Cl₂ |
| Ar | single atoms | very weak van der Waals forces | lowest |
- **Na to Al.** The ions carry more charge (1+, 2+, 3+) and are smaller. There are more delocalised electrons per atom, so the electrostatic attraction is stronger and the melting point rises.
- **Si** has a giant covalent lattice, so a lot of energy is needed to break its covalent bonds.
- **S₈ > P₄ > Cl₂ > Ar.** Bigger molecules have more electrons, which gives stronger van der Waals forces.
:::ex Explaining a melting point difference
Q: Explain why sulfur has a higher melting point than phosphorus.
1. Both are simple molecular: sulfur exists as S₈ and phosphorus as P₄.
2. S₈ has more electrons than P₄, so the van der Waals forces between its molecules are stronger.
3. More energy is needed to overcome them.
A: The S₈ molecules are larger, so they have stronger van der Waals forces than P₄ molecules.
:::
:::ex Explaining an ionisation energy difference
Q: Explain why the first ionisation energy of magnesium is greater than that of sodium.
1. Mg has one more proton than Na.
2. Its outer electrons are in the same 3s sub-shell, with similar shielding.
3. Mg also has a smaller atomic radius, so its outer electron is attracted more strongly.
A: Mg has a greater nuclear charge, similar shielding and a smaller radius, so more energy is needed to remove its outer electron.
:::
:::warn
- Saying that atomic radius decreases because "there are more electrons". The key is the increasing **nuclear charge** with the **same shielding**.
- Saying that silicon's covalent bonds break as "intermolecular forces". Silicon is giant covalent; it has no molecules.
- Forgetting the molecular formulae P₄, S₈ and Cl₂ when explaining melting points.
:::
:::tip
- In periodicity answers, structure the explanation as structure, then bonding or force, then strength, then energy needed.
- For IE exceptions, give the sub-shell (3s compared with 3p) or the pairing in 3p⁴.
:::
:::sum
- Blocks: s, p, d and f, based on the highest-energy sub-shell.
- Across Period 3: the radius decreases and the first IE increases, with dips at Al and S.
- Melting point: rises from Na to Al (metallic), peaks at Si (giant covalent), is low for P₄, S₈ and Cl₂ (molecular), and lowest for Ar.
:::

# Enthalpy
- ΔH is the heat energy change at constant pressure. Exothermic: ΔH < 0. Standard conditions: 100 kPa and a stated temperature (usually 298 K).
- $\Delta_cH^\circ$: 1 mol burned completely in oxygen. $\Delta_fH^\circ$: 1 mol formed from its elements in their standard states.
!fig c-profiles
# Calorimetry
$$q=mc\Delta T$$
$$\Delta H=-\frac{q}{n}$$
- $m$ is the mass of water or solution (not the fuel). $c=4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$.
# Hess's law and bond enthalpies
- The enthalpy change is independent of the route taken.
$$\Delta_rH=\sum\Delta_fH_{\text{prod}}-\sum\Delta_fH_{\text{react}}$$
$$\Delta_rH=\sum\Delta_cH_{\text{react}}-\sum\Delta_cH_{\text{prod}}$$
$$\Delta H=\sum(\text{bonds broken})-\sum(\text{bonds made})$$
- Mean bond enthalpies are averages over many compounds, so results are less accurate.
!fig c-hess
# Worked example
50.0 g of water rises by 12.0 K when 0.0100 mol of fuel burns: $q=50.0\times4.18\times12.0=2508$ J, so $\Delta_cH=-251\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
# Exam tips
> Convert J to kJ and include the **sign**. Reasons a value is less exothermic than the data book: heat loss, incomplete combustion, non-standard conditions.

@intro Redox reactions involve transfer of electrons. This topic covers oxidation states, identifying oxidising and reducing agents, and writing and combining half-equations, including those in acidic solution.
# Oxidation and reduction
:::def
**Oxidation:** loss of electrons, or an increase in oxidation state.
**Reduction:** gain of electrons, or a decrease in oxidation state.
**Oxidising agent:** an electron acceptor. It is reduced itself.
**Reducing agent:** an electron donor. It is oxidised itself.
:::
> Remember **OIL RIG**: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).
# Oxidation states
Rules, in order of priority:
1. An uncombined element is 0, for example Na, O₂ and Cl₂.
2. The oxidation states in a neutral compound add up to 0. In an ion they add up to the ion's charge.
3. A simple ion has the same oxidation state as its charge: Na⁺ is +1, Cl⁻ is −1.
4. Group 1 is +1, Group 2 is +2 and Al is +3. F is always −1.
5. H is +1, except in metal hydrides such as NaH, where it is −1.
6. O is −2, except in peroxides such as H₂O₂, where it is −1, and in OF₂, where it is +2.
7. Cl is −1, except when it is combined with O or F.
- Roman numerals in names give the oxidation state: iron(III) is Fe³⁺, and in chlorate(I), ClO⁻, Cl is +1.
:::ex Finding oxidation states
Q: Find the oxidation state of Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻ and of S in S₂O₃²⁻.
1. Cr₂O₇²⁻: $2x+7(-2)=-2$, so $2x=12$ and $x=+6$.
2. S₂O₃²⁻: $2x+3(-2)=-2$, so $2x=4$ and $x=+2$.
A: Cr is +6; S is +2.
:::
# Half-equations
A **half-equation** shows the electrons gained or lost by one species, for example Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ or Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻.
In acidic solution, balance a half-equation in this order:
1. Balance the element that changes oxidation state.
2. Balance O by adding **H₂O**.
3. Balance H by adding **H⁺**.
4. Balance charge by adding **electrons** to the more positive side.
:::ex Manganate(VII) half-equation
Q: Write the half-equation for MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺ in acid.
1. Mn is balanced. Add 4H₂O on the right to balance the 4 O atoms.
2. Add 8H⁺ on the left to balance the H.
3. Charge on the left is −1 + 8 = +7; charge on the right is +2. Add 5e⁻ to the left.
A: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O
:::
# Combining half-equations
1. Multiply the half-equations so that the numbers of electrons are equal.
2. Add them together and cancel the electrons, plus any H⁺ or H₂O that appear on both sides.
:::ex Overall redox equation
Q: Combine MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O with Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻.
1. Multiply the iron half-equation by 5: 5Fe²⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + 5e⁻.
2. Add the two and cancel the 5e⁻.
A: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺
:::
- Check the final equation: the atoms balance, the total charge is the same on both sides (+17 on each side here), and no electrons are left.
- **Disproportionation** is when the same element is both oxidised and reduced. An example is Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O, where Cl goes from 0 to −1 and +1.
:::warn
- Writing electrons on the wrong side. In an oxidation half-equation the electrons are **products**.
- Giving oxidation states as charges ("2+") instead of "+2". Write the sign first.
- Saying the oxidising agent is oxidised. It is **reduced**.
:::
:::tip
- To identify what is oxidised and what is reduced, write the oxidation states above every element in the equation and look for the changes.
- In combined equations, check both atoms and charges. Examiners check both.
:::
:::sum
- OIL RIG. An oxidising agent gains electrons; a reducing agent loses electrons.
- Assign oxidation states using the priority rules (F, then O, then H).
- Balance half-equations in acid with H₂O, then H⁺, then e⁻. Combine by matching the electrons.
:::

@intro The Group 2 metals (Be to Ba) show clear trends in reactivity and in the solubility of their compounds. Several of the compounds have important uses in medicine, farming and industry.
# Physical trends down the group
| Property | Trend down Group 2 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| atomic radius | increases | an extra shell of electrons each time |
| first ionisation energy | decreases | the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, which outweighs the extra nuclear charge |
| melting point | generally decreases | Group 2 elements have giant **metallic** structures. The metal ions get larger while the charge stays 2+, so the attraction to the delocalised electrons weakens (Mg is an anomaly because its crystal structure is different) |
# Reactions with water
- Reactivity increases down the group, because the first and second ionisation energies decrease, so the atoms lose their two outer electrons more easily.
- **Magnesium** reacts very slowly with cold water: Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂. It burns in **steam** with a bright white flame: Mg(s) + H₂O(g) → MgO(s) + H₂(g).
- Calcium, strontium and barium react with cold water with increasing vigour: M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂. The resulting solution is alkaline.
# Solubility trends
| Compound | Trend down the group | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| hydroxides M(OH)₂ | solubility **increases** | Mg(OH)₂ is sparingly soluble; Ba(OH)₂ is quite soluble |
| sulfates MSO₄ | solubility **decreases** | MgSO₄ is soluble; BaSO₄ is insoluble |
# Uses of Group 2 metals and compounds
- **Mg in the extraction of titanium:** TiCl₄ + 2Mg → Ti + 2MgCl₂. This is done at high temperature in an argon atmosphere.
- **Mg(OH)₂ (milk of magnesia)** is an antacid, neutralising excess stomach acid: Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H₂O.
- **Ca(OH)₂ (slaked lime)** is used in agriculture to neutralise acidic soils.
- **CaO and CaCO₃ remove SO₂ from flue gases** (flue gas desulfurisation): CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃ and CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂.
- **BaSO₄ in barium meals.** BaSO₄ is insoluble, so the toxic Ba²⁺ ions are not absorbed by the body. Barium absorbs X-rays strongly, so the gut shows up on an X-ray.
- **Acidified BaCl₂ tests for sulfate ions.** A white precipitate of BaSO₄ forms: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s). The solution is first acidified with HCl to remove carbonate ions, which would also give a white precipitate.
:::ex Writing an equation for flue gas desulfurisation
Q: Write an equation for the reaction of calcium carbonate with sulfur dioxide, and explain why this is useful.
1. The products are calcium sulfite and carbon dioxide.
2. CaCO₃(s) + SO₂(g) → CaSO₃(s) + CO₂(g).
A: This removes SO₂ from power-station emissions, which reduces acid rain.
:::
:::ex Explaining the use of barium sulfate
Q: Barium compounds are toxic. Explain why a barium meal is safe to swallow.
1. Barium sulfate is insoluble in water.
2. So almost no Ba²⁺ ions dissolve or pass into the blood.
A: BaSO₄ is insoluble, so toxic Ba²⁺ ions are not absorbed, while barium still absorbs X-rays to outline the gut.
:::
:::prac Required practical 4: test-tube tests for ions
- **Sulfate:** add HCl(aq), then BaCl₂(aq). A white precipitate shows SO₄²⁻.
- **Group 2 cations:** add NaOH(aq). Mg²⁺ gives a white precipitate of Mg(OH)₂. Ba²⁺ gives little or no precipitate, because Ba(OH)₂ is soluble.
- **Hydroxide:** the solution turns red litmus blue.
- **Carbonate:** add dilute acid. Effervescence occurs, and the gas turns limewater milky.
- **Ammonium:** warm with NaOH(aq). Ammonia is given off, which turns damp red litmus blue.
:::
:::warn
- Saying that "Group 2 compounds" get more or less soluble without saying which compound. Hydroxides become **more** soluble down the group; sulfates become **less** soluble.
- Forgetting to acidify before the BaCl₂ test.
- Saying that magnesium reacts with steam to give Mg(OH)₂. It gives **MgO** and H₂.
:::
:::tip
- Learn one balanced equation for each use: Ti extraction, an antacid, flue gas desulfurisation and the sulfate test.
:::
:::sum
- Down Group 2: the radius increases, the IE decreases, the reactivity with water increases, and the melting point generally decreases.
- Hydroxides become more soluble down the group; sulfates become less soluble.
- Uses: Mg for Ti, Mg(OH)₂ as an antacid, Ca(OH)₂ for soil, CaO/CaCO₃ to remove SO₂, BaSO₄ for X-rays, and BaCl₂ to test for sulfate.
:::

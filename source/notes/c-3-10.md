@intro Benzene, C₆H₆, is unusually stable because of its ring of delocalised electrons. Unlike alkenes, it reacts by electrophilic substitution, which keeps the stable ring intact.
# Bonding in benzene
- Benzene is a **planar hexagonal** molecule. Each carbon forms three σ bonds (to two carbons and one hydrogen), with bond angles of 120°.
- Each carbon's remaining **p orbital** overlaps with those of its neighbours, forming a ring of **delocalised π electrons** above and below the plane.
- **Evidence against the Kekulé structure**, which has three alternating C=C bonds:
1. All six C–C bonds are the **same length** (0.139 nm), between a C–C single bond (0.154 nm) and a C=C double bond (0.134 nm).
2. **Enthalpy of hydrogenation.** Cyclohexene gives −120 kJ mol⁻¹, so Kekulé benzene would be expected to give 3 × −120 = −360 kJ mol⁻¹. The measured value is only about **−208 kJ mol⁻¹**. Benzene is about **152 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable** than predicted.
3. Benzene does not decolourise bromine water under normal conditions, so it does not undergo addition.
- **Electrophilic substitution** is preferred to addition because substitution keeps the delocalised ring, and with it the extra stability.
:::ex Delocalisation energy
Q: Hydrogenating cyclohexa-1,3-diene gives ΔH = −232 kJ mol⁻¹, and hydrogenating benzene gives −208 kJ mol⁻¹. Estimate the stabilisation of benzene relative to Kekulé benzene, using −120 kJ mol⁻¹ per C=C.
1. Kekulé benzene would give 3 × (−120) = −360 kJ mol⁻¹.
2. The difference is −208 − (−360) = +152 kJ mol⁻¹.
A: Benzene is about 152 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable. (The diene value, −232 ≈ 2 × −120, confirms that the per-C=C figure is sensible.)
:::
# Nitration
- Reagents: **concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄**, at **50 °C** or below. Higher temperatures give multiple substitution.
- **Generating the electrophile:** HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → **NO₂⁺** + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻.
!fig c-nitration Nitration of benzene: electrophilic substitution
**Mechanism:**
1. An arrow goes from the delocalised ring to the N of NO₂⁺.
2. This forms an intermediate with a partial (broken) delocalised ring and a + charge, with the H and NO₂ on the same carbon.
3. The C–H bond electrons return to the ring, re-forming the delocalised system, and H⁺ is lost. The H⁺ re-forms H₂SO₄ with HSO₄⁻, so H₂SO₄ acts as a catalyst.
- **Importance:** nitration is used to make **explosives** such as TNT, and to make **aromatic amines** (by reduction of nitro compounds), which are used to make dyes.
# Friedel–Crafts acylation
- Reagents: an **acyl chloride** (RCOCl) with an **AlCl₃ catalyst**, heated under reflux in a dry solvent. The product is a **phenyl ketone**, for example C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl.
- **Generating the electrophile:** RCOCl + AlCl₃ → **RCO⁺** (the acylium ion) + AlCl₄⁻.
- The mechanism is the same pattern as nitration. Then H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl, which regenerates the catalyst.
- Acylation is important because it makes C–C bonds to the ring, which is useful in synthesis.
:::ex Friedel–Crafts product
Q: Name the organic product of benzene with propanoyl chloride and AlCl₃, and give the electrophile.
1. The electrophile is CH₃CH₂CO⁺, formed from CH₃CH₂COCl + AlCl₃.
2. It substitutes for one H on the ring.
A: 1-phenylpropan-1-one, C₆H₅COCH₂CH₃.
:::
:::warn
- Drawing the intermediate with a full delocalised circle. It must be a **partial** ring (a horseshoe) open at the sp³ carbon, with a + charge.
- Writing addition products for benzene.
- Forgetting to show catalyst regeneration in equations.
:::
:::tip
- Learn the three electrophile-forming equations (for NO₂⁺, RCO⁺ and AlCl₄⁻).
- The horseshoe should cover 5 carbons and be open towards the carbon carrying the H and the new group.
:::
:::sum
- Benzene is planar with delocalised π electrons; its bond lengths and its hydrogenation enthalpy disprove the Kekulé structure.
- Nitration uses conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄ at 50 °C with the NO₂⁺ electrophile; it is used for explosives and amines.
- Friedel–Crafts acylation uses RCOCl/AlCl₃ with the RCO⁺ electrophile and gives phenyl ketones.
- Substitution keeps the stable ring.
:::

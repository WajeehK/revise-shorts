@intro Electric fields are closely parallel to gravitational fields, but they can repel as well as attract. This topic covers Coulomb's law, uniform fields between plates, the deflection of charged particles, and electric potential.
# Electric fields
:::def
**Electric field strength (E):** the force per unit positive charge, $E=F/Q$ (in N C⁻¹, or V m⁻¹).
:::
- Field lines run from + to −. A charged sphere behaves as if its charge were at its centre.
!fig p-efield-radial Radial field around a point charge
# Coulomb's law
:::eq
$$F=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}\qquad E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}\qquad\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\text{ F m}^{-1}$$
:::
- Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. The force is an inverse-square law, just like gravity.
:::ex Force in a hydrogen atom
Q: Find the force between a proton and an electron 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m apart.
1. F = (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × (5.3 × 10⁻¹¹)²).
A: F = 8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N (attractive), which is about 10³⁹ times the gravitational force between them.
:::
# Uniform fields and capacitors
!fig p-efield-plates Uniform field between parallel plates
:::eq
$$E=\frac{V}{d}\qquad C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}\ \ (\text{or }\frac{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_rA}{d}\text{ with a dielectric})$$
:::
- A dielectric increases C, by a factor of the **relative permittivity**, ε_r. Its polar molecules align with the field.
:::ex Parallel plates
Q: Two plates 2.0 mm apart have an area of 0.040 m² and a p.d. of 500 V. Find E, and C with air between the plates.
1. E = 500 / 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 2.5 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹.
2. C = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.040 / 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ F.
A: E = 2.5 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹ and C = 180 pF
:::
# Charged particles in a uniform field
!fig p-electron-deflect An electron follows a parabolic path between deflecting plates
- A charged particle entering at right angles to the field feels a constant force, F = EQ, perpendicular to its velocity. Its path is a **parabola**, just like a projectile.
- Horizontal velocity is constant. The vertical acceleration is a = EQ/m.
:::ex Deflection of an electron
Q: An electron enters a 2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ field at 3.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹, at right angles to the field. The plates are 0.050 m long. Find its vertical deflection.
1. Time between the plates: t = 0.050 / 3.0 × 10⁷ = 1.67 × 10⁻⁹ s.
2. a = eE/m = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁴ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 3.5 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻².
3. y = ½at² = ½ × 3.5 × 10¹⁵ × (1.67 × 10⁻⁹)².
A: y = 4.9 × 10⁻³ m (about 5 mm), towards the positive plate.
:::
# Electric potential and energy
:::eq
$$V=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}\qquad E_p=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}$$
:::
- **Electric potential** is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to that point. It is positive near a positive charge.
- The area under an F–r graph is the work done. The capacitance of an isolated sphere is C = 4πε₀R.
:::ex Closest approach
Q: An alpha particle (q = +2e) with 5.0 MeV of kinetic energy approaches a gold nucleus (Q = +79e). Find its distance of closest approach.
1. At closest approach, all the KE has become potential energy: 5.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹³ J.
2. r = Qq / (4πε₀ × KE) = (79 × 2 × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)²) / (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 8.0 × 10⁻¹³).
A: r = 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m, which sets an upper limit on the size of the nucleus.
:::
:::warn
- Using the 1/r² law for potential. Potential goes as **1/r**, and the field as 1/r².
- Forgetting that E = V/d applies only to **uniform** fields.
- Assuming the electric force is always attractive, as gravity is.
:::
:::tip
- Make a table comparing gravitational and electric fields (force, field strength, potential and their units). Examiners love that comparison.
:::
:::sum
- E = F/Q. Coulomb: F = Qq/4πε₀r²; radial E = Q/4πε₀r².
- Uniform field: E = V/d; C = ε₀ε_rA/d; charged particles follow parabolic paths.
- V = Q/4πε₀r; E_p = Qq/4πε₀r.
:::

@intro Transition metals (Ti to Cu) have partly filled d sub-shells, which gives them their characteristic properties: complex ions, colours, variable oxidation states and catalytic activity. This is one of the largest topics in the course.
# General properties
:::def
**Transition metal:** an element that forms at least one stable ion with an **incomplete d sub-shell**. Sc and Zn are not included: Sc³⁺ is 3d⁰ and Zn²⁺ is 3d¹⁰.
:::
- The four characteristic properties are **complex formation**, **coloured ions**, **variable oxidation states** and **catalytic activity**. They all come from the incomplete d sub-shell.
# Complexes, ligands and shapes
:::def
**Ligand:** a molecule or ion that donates a lone pair of electrons to a central metal ion, forming a co-ordinate bond.
**Complex:** a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands.
**Co-ordination number:** the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal ion.
:::
- **Monodentate** ligands (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻) form one bond. **Bidentate** ligands (ethane-1,2-diamine, written en; and ethanedioate, C₂O₄²⁻) form two. **Multidentate** EDTA⁴⁻ forms six.
- **Shapes:** octahedral with 6 small ligands, for example [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Tetrahedral with 4 larger Cl⁻ ligands, for example [CuCl₄]²⁻. Square planar, for example cisplatin [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]. Linear, for example [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ in Tollens' reagent.
!fig c-complexes Shapes of complex ions
- **Isomerism.** Square planar and octahedral complexes can show **cis–trans isomerism**: cisplatin is the cis isomer. Octahedral complexes with three bidentate ligands, such as [Ni(en)₃]²⁺, show **optical isomerism**, existing as non-superimposable mirror images.
- **Haem** is an iron(II) complex with a multidentate ligand. O₂ binds to the Fe²⁺ in haemoglobin. **CO is toxic** because it binds more strongly than O₂ and replaces it.
# Ligand substitution and the chelate effect
- H₂O and NH₃ are similar in size, so substitution keeps the **co-ordination number at 6**: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O. With copper the substitution is partial: [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O, giving a deep blue solution.
- Cl⁻ is larger, so the co-ordination number **changes from 6 to 4**: [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O. The colour goes from blue to yellow-green, and the shape from octahedral to tetrahedral.
- **The chelate effect.** Multidentate ligands form more stable complexes. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O turns 2 particles into 7, so **entropy increases** greatly (ΔS is positive). ΔH is close to zero because similar bonds are broken and made, so ΔG is negative.
# Formation of coloured ions
- In a complex, the ligands split the d orbitals into two energy levels. An electron absorbs a photon to jump up the gap, ΔE, moving from the **ground state** to an **excited state**. The colour we see is the light that is **not** absorbed (the complementary colour).
$$\Delta E=h\nu=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
- The colour changes with **oxidation state**, **co-ordination number** and **ligand**, because each of these changes ΔE.
- This absorption of visible light is the basis of **spectroscopy** (UV–visible spectroscopy) for transition-metal ions.
- **Colorimetry.** Measure the absorbance of standard solutions and plot a calibration curve. Then read the concentration of the unknown from it. Use a filter of the complementary colour.
# Variable oxidation states
- **Vanadium.** Zinc in acid reduces vanadium step by step: VO₂⁺ (V, yellow) → VO²⁺ (IV, blue) → V³⁺ (III, green) → V²⁺ (II, violet).
- The redox potential of a transition metal ion depends on **pH** and on the **ligand**. For example, Cr(III) is oxidised to Cr(VI) more easily in alkali, and silver forms the stable complex [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.
- **Tollens' reagent** is [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. It is reduced to silver by aldehydes, giving a silver mirror.
- **Redox titrations** use KMnO₄ in acid, which is its own indicator: the end point is the first permanent pale pink.
:::eq
$$\text{MnO}_4^-+8\text{H}^++5\text{Fe}^{2+}\rightarrow\text{Mn}^{2+}+4\text{H}_2\text{O}+5\text{Fe}^{3+}$$
$$2\text{MnO}_4^-+16\text{H}^++5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}\rightarrow2\text{Mn}^{2+}+8\text{H}_2\text{O}+10\text{CO}_2$$
:::
:::ex Redox titration
Q: 25.0 cm³ of Fe²⁺(aq) needs 21.60 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. Find [Fe²⁺].
1. Moles of MnO₄⁻ $=0.0200\times0.02160=4.32\times10^{-4}$ mol.
2. The ratio is 1 MnO₄⁻ : 5 Fe²⁺, so moles of Fe²⁺ $=2.16\times10^{-3}$ mol.
3. $[\text{Fe}^{2+}]=2.16\times10^{-3}/0.0250$.
A: 0.0864 mol dm⁻³
:::
:::ex Explaining a colour
Q: Explain why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is blue.
1. Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, an incomplete d sub-shell. The water ligands split the d orbitals.
2. An electron absorbs light in the red–orange region to jump across the gap ΔE.
3. The remaining wavelengths are transmitted or reflected.
A: The ion absorbs red/orange light, so we see the complementary colour, blue.
:::
# Catalysts
- **Heterogeneous** catalysts are in a different phase from the reactants. Reactants adsorb onto active sites, bonds weaken, the reaction happens, and the products desorb.
  - **Contact process:** V₂O₅ catalyses the conversion of SO₂ to SO₃. Step 1: SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄. Step 2: V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅.
  - **Haber process:** iron catalyses the formation of NH₃.
  - Catalysts are spread on a **support** (for example a ceramic honeycomb) to maximise the surface area and reduce cost. They are **poisoned** by impurities that block the active sites, for example sulfur on the iron in the Haber process, or lead in catalytic converters.
- **Homogeneous** catalysts are in the same phase. They work through an intermediate species.
  - Fe²⁺ catalyses S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂. The two negative ions repel each other, so the direct reaction is slow. Instead: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺, then 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂.
  - **Autocatalysis:** Mn²⁺, a product, catalyses the reaction between MnO₄⁻ and C₂O₄²⁻. The reaction starts slowly, then speeds up.
:::warn
- Calling Zn a transition metal. Its only ion, Zn²⁺, has a full d sub-shell.
- Saying a complex is coloured because it "emits" light. It **absorbs** some wavelengths; we see the rest.
- Writing the co-ordination number as the number of ligands. For bidentate ligands it is double the number of ligands.
:::
:::tip
- For the chelate effect, always give the change in the number of particles, the sign of ΔS, ΔH ≈ 0, and therefore ΔG < 0.
- Learn the vanadium colours in order: yellow, blue, green, violet.
:::
:::sum
- A transition metal forms an ion with an incomplete d sub-shell. Its properties are complexes, colour, variable oxidation states and catalysis.
- Ligands donate lone pairs. Shapes are octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar or linear. Multidentate ligands are more stable (the chelate effect, from entropy).
- Colour comes from d-electron transitions: ΔE = hν, and it changes with oxidation state, ligand and co-ordination number.
- KMnO₄ titrations: 1 MnO₄⁻ reacts with 5 Fe²⁺.
- Heterogeneous catalysts work by adsorption (V₂O₅, Fe); homogeneous ones work through intermediates (Fe²⁺ with S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻); Mn²⁺ is an autocatalyst.
:::

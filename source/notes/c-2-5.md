# What a transition metal is
- It forms at least one stable ion with a partially filled d sub-shell. Properties: complexes, coloured ions, variable oxidation states, catalysis.
# Complexes
- Ligand: donates a lone pair to form a coordinate (dative) bond. Monodentate: H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻. Bidentate: en, C₂O₄²⁻. Multidentate: EDTA⁴⁻ (hexadentate).
- Shapes: octahedral (6 small ligands), tetrahedral (4 Cl⁻, e.g. [CuCl₄]²⁻), square planar (cisplatin), linear ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺).
- Isomerism: cis/trans (octahedral and square planar); optical (with 3 bidentate ligands).
- **Chelate effect:** multidentate ligands substitute monodentate ones because the number of particles increases, so ΔS > 0.
- Haem contains Fe²⁺. CO binds more strongly than O₂, which is why CO is toxic.

# Colour
$$\Delta E=hf=\frac{hc}\lambda$$
- The d orbitals split. Electrons absorb visible light and are promoted; the complementary colour is seen. Colour depends on the metal, its oxidation state, the ligands and the coordination number.
- Colorimetry: measure absorbance against concentration with a calibration curve.
# Redox titrations and catalysis
- MnO₄⁻ (self-indicating, colourless → pale pink). MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5. Cr₂O₇²⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 6.
- Heterogeneous catalysis: V₂O₅ (Contact process), Fe (Haber). Homogeneous: Fe²⁺ for S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻. Autocatalysis: Mn²⁺ in MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻.
# Exam tips
> Zn and Sc are d-block but **not** transition metals: Zn²⁺ is d¹⁰ and Sc³⁺ is d⁰.

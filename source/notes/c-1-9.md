# Rate equations
$$\text{rate}=k[\text{A}]^m[\text{B}]^n$$
- Orders are found by **experiment**, not from the equation. The overall order is $m+n$.
- Order 0: rate is independent of concentration. Order 1: rate ∝ concentration. Order 2: doubling the concentration gives 4× the rate.
- Concentration–time graphs: order 0 is a straight line; order 1 has a constant half-life. Rate–concentration graphs: order 0 is horizontal, order 1 is a straight line through the origin, order 2 is a curve.
- Units of $k$ come from rearranging, e.g. for a 2nd-order reaction: mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹.

# Arrhenius
$$k=Ae^{-E_a/RT}$$
$$\ln k=\ln A-\frac{E_a}{R}\cdot\frac1T$$
- A plot of $\ln k$ against $\frac1T$ has gradient $-\frac{E_a}R$.
# Rate-determining step
- The slowest step. Species in the rate equation appear in (or before) the RDS.
# Worked example
A gradient of $-6000$ K gives $E_a=6000\times8.31=49.9\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
# Exam tips
> Initial rates come from the tangent at $t=0$. A clock reaction gives rate ∝ $\frac1t$.

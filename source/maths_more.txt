# s-samp|1: Statistical sampling|Statistics
1M|The whole group you want to study is the|population|sample|census|frame|The population is the whole group of interest. A sample is a selected subset of it.
1M|Surveying every member of a population is a|census|sample|quota|stratum|A census measures every member of the population. It is accurate but can be time-consuming and expensive, and it is not practical if testing destroys items.
1W|Give one advantage of a sample over a census.|cheaper;quicker;faster;less time;easier;less data|A sample is cheaper and quicker than a census and gives less data to process. It is also essential when testing destroys the items.
1M|Each item is numbered and chosen with random numbers. This is|simple random sampling|systematic sampling|quota sampling|cluster sampling|Simple random sampling: every sample of the given size is equally likely. It needs a sampling frame so each item can be numbered.
1M|Which sampling method is NOT random?|Opportunity|Simple random|Systematic (random start)|Stratified|Opportunity (convenience) sampling uses whoever is available at the time, so it is not random and is likely to be biased.
2N|A factory has 120 day-shift and 80 night-shift workers. A stratified sample of 30 is taken. How many from night shift?|12|0||Night shift is 80 of the 200 workers, so take 80/200 × 30 = 12 from the night shift (and 18 from the day shift).|80/200*30
2N|Systematic sample of 20 from a list of 500. What is the interval k?|25|0||k = population size ÷ sample size = 500 ÷ 20 = 25. Choose a random start from 1 to 25, then take every 25th item.|500/20
2M|A disadvantage of opportunity sampling is that it|is unlikely to represent the population|needs a sampling frame|is expensive|takes a long time|The sample is chosen for convenience, so it is likely to be biased and unrepresentative.
2M|Stratified sampling is useful when|the population splits into clear groups|there is no sampling frame|the population is tiny|data is qualitative only|Stratified sampling is used when the population splits into distinct groups (strata). Sampling each group in proportion makes the sample representative.
2W|What is a sampling unit?|individual;item;member;one|A sampling unit is a single member (individual or item) of the population that can be selected.
2M|In the Large Data Set, 'tr' for rainfall means|trace: less than 0.05 mm|missing data|thunderstorm|treble|'tr' means trace: less than 0.05 mm of rain. It is usually treated as 0 in calculations.
2M|Quota sampling is often used in|market research interviews|lotteries|quality control by random numbers|censuses|Market research interviewers are given quotas (e.g. 20 men aged 18–30) and fill them with people they meet. It is quick but not random.

3W|Why might a systematic sample be biased?|pattern;periodic;cycle;repeat|If the list has a repeating pattern that matches the interval k, the sample may pick the same type of item every time, so it is biased.
3M|Cluster sampling involves|randomly choosing whole groups, then sampling within them|choosing every kth member|choosing people available|interviewing quotas|Cluster sampling divides the population into groups (clusters), randomly chooses some whole clusters and samples within them. 2M|Which method gives each member an equal chance and does not divide the population into groups?|Simple random sampling|Quota sampling|Opportunity sampling|Judgement sampling|Simple random sampling: every member is equally likely and no division into strata is needed.
1M|A sample should be|representative of the population|as small as possible|chosen by the researcher's preference|the whole population|A sample should represent the population so that conclusions from it can be generalised to the whole population.
1W|What is bias in sampling?|not representative;unrepresentative;favour;systematic;skew|Bias occurs when the sampling method systematically favours some members, so the sample does not represent the population.
